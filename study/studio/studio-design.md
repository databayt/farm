# Studio design

Status: Layout B, chosen by the founder on 2026-10-26 from two reference images (D-020). It replaces Layout A (D-015) and keeps its bathroom, which survives the photo tolerance.

## Brief

- Rentable farm-stay units for two guests: hotel-grade but durable, easy to clean between stays.
- Bed, shower, WC, basin and a kitchen that guests will actually cook in; no gas inside a steel box.
- All three identical, so one kit design, one cutting list, one manual.
- Built from materials sold in Kigali, cut and drilled in a Kigali workshop, assembled on site by a small crew.
- Keep the existing openings: a 3 m sliding glass wall and a narrow steel door.

## Options considered

![A-102 Layout options](../../public/designs/studio/drawings/a-102.svg)

Layout B reads like a small apartment in three zones, after the founder's reference plan: a bedroom with a storage wall, a living room with a sofa, and a galley with the bathroom behind it. The bathroom stays at the narrow-door end, and that door becomes a frosted louvre window (W1), as in Layout A. The galley runs 1.45 m along the bathroom partition, from the back wall to the bathroom door: an under-counter fridge, a two-zone induction hob and a sink, with an overhead cabinet that holds the 80 L heater lying on a steel cradle and a hood beneath it. The sink, the heater, the shower, the basin and the WC still share one wet core and one drain exit. The bed goes across the quiet far end, with open shelf towers either side of the headboard and a picture niche between them. The middle is a seating room: a compact two-seat sofa on the back wall, a table in front of it and two chairs facing it from the glass side. Guests enter through the sliding glass from the deck.

The reference room is 2.9 m deep; the container is 2.25 m inside, so three things changed to fit: one table and two chairs serve as both lounge and dining, instead of a coffee table plus a dining table for four; the wardrobe gives way to the shelf towers, coat hooks and a luggage rack; and the bed has no drawers, because the sofa and the chairs stand beside it (lift the deck to reach the storage underneath).

A first version faced the WC along the container. The generator showed it failed if the glazing ended more than about 70 mm later than the photo suggests. The final arrangement faces the WC across the width, on the infill below the old door, and passes across the whole plus or minus 150 mm band. Similar layouts are common in 20 ft precedents: a bath, kitchen and bed in a row [V STU-46], a full-width bathroom [V STU-47], a bed across the end in container hotels [V STU-49] and a raised bed facing large glass [V STU-48].

The partition's living face sits 30 mm past the end of the glazing, on the strip of solid steel before the old door. If the survey moves the glazing, the partition and the galley move with it, and the galley aisle takes up the difference: about 980 mm from the worktop to the sofa at the photo estimate, 830 mm if the glazing ends 150 mm earlier. The build fails, rather than drawing something unbuildable, if any keep-out zone is blocked: the WC's 600 mm front zone, the shower entry, the door and drawer sweeps, and an 800 mm galley aisle.

## Finishes

| Surface | Finish | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Walls and ceiling (living) | 12 mm ply, light PU varnish (vapour-retarding) | Warm, durable, repairable; seals the insulation from room air |
| Floor (living) | Epoxy seal over the treated container floor, then 5 mm SPC | The original floor is insecticide-treated; never sand it [V STU-07] |
| Bathroom walls | Cement board, liquid membrane, ceramic tiles | Fully waterproof; easy to clean between guests |
| Bathroom floor | Safety vinyl coved 100 mm up the walls, low-profile tray | No joints at the floor; the tray keeps IP zone 1 small [V STU-38] |
| Furniture | 18 mm ply or MR melamine board, ABS edges; marine ply in the bathroom | Whatever Kigali stocks; both are drawn |
| Worktop | 38 mm laminate or hardwood | Field check (AS-24) |
| Outside | Existing paint kept; steelwork for the roof painted to match | Founder did not ask for a repaint |
