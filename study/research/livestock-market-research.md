# Goat and sheep market, breeds and pasture

Status: Stage 1 desk research — preliminary  
Researched: 2026-10-19  
Location dependency: High. The farm location is unconfirmed [U], so nothing here is site-specific. The eastern lowland candidates (Lake Rumira/Gashora in Bugesera; the Muhazi shore; Mugesera in Ngoma/Rwamagana) are goat country and were under the 2026 Rift Valley fever (RVF) quarantine; Bugesera is semi-arid. The western highland candidate (Shyira, Nyabihu) is cooler and wetter, sheep-friendly, near the DRC border market, and was not named as quarantined in August 2026. Where findings differ by zone the text says so (see section 3.2).

Evidence labels: [V] verified (source ID), [E] estimate (method shown), [A] assumption (validation given), [U] unknown. Prices are RWF unless stated. All sources were accessed 2026-10-19. Names and mobile numbers of private individuals printed in public reports are deliberately not copied.

## At a glance

- **2026 disease shock, Eastern Province.** [V] On 2026-06-26 RAB quarantined cattle, goats and sheep in six districts (Rulindo, Burera, Gicumbi, Kirehe, Kayonza, Rwamagana), then the whole Eastern Province (which includes Bugesera and Ngoma) and parts of the Northern Province. Animals had to stay where reared, slaughter and meat sales were suspended, and all had to be vaccinated within 7 days (LVS-03, LVS-04). On 2026-08-21 only Nyagatare, Gatsibo, Ngoma and Kirehe markets were reopening (12 market days to 2026-09-28); Bugesera, Rwamagana and Kayonza markets stayed closed (LVS-05). [U] Status after 2026-09-28. Western Province was not named as quarantined (LVS-04).
- **Demand: goat far ahead of sheep, hospitality-led, spiky.** [V] Goat is 19% of Rwanda's meat output and sheep 5% (LVS-02). Households do not eat goat regularly; the biggest buyers are hotels, bars and restaurants (brochettes) and households at festive seasons (LVS-01). Saban abattoir (Nyabugogo) slaughtered about 150 goats and 40 sheep a day in 2019, rising roughly 4x and 3x at New Year; mutton "attracts the least customers" (LVS-07). Exception: DRC traders pay a premium for Nyabihu mutton (LVS-56).
- **Prices.** [V] Kigali retail goat-and-sheep meat Rwf7,500 to 8,000/kg in July 2025, up from Rwf6,000 a year earlier (LVS-02), against Rwf3,500 for goat meat in January 2018 (LVS-53). Live goats, 2022 district survey: Bugesera kid 20,000, medium animal 40,000, big animal 60,000; Kayonza 18,000 / 35,000 / 50,000 (LVS-01). A 30 to 35 kg sheep sold for Rwf100,000 at Mukamira (Nyabihu) in May 2024 (LVS-56). [U] No Kigali live price, no live goat price after 2022 and no price during the 2026 outbreak was found.
- **Stock numbers are uncertain and falling.** [V] Goats: NISR AHS 2020 counted 1.75 million; the district-reported MINAGRI series gives 2.73 million (2018) and 2.84 million (2020); MINAGRI's 2022/23 report 2.00 million. Sheep: 0.42 million (AHS 2020), 0.60 million (district series 2018 and 2020), 0.44 million (2022/23) (LVS-01, LVS-08, LVS-49, LVS-51). Every series is below 2016/17 (about 2.9 million goats, 0.72 million sheep). MINAGRI blames poor local breeds, inbreeding, feed, disease and a disorganised market (LVS-08).
- **Zone fit.** [V] 2018 district counts: Bugesera 132,556 goats and 6,744 sheep; Nyabihu 84,121 goats and 30,792 sheep (LVS-51). The Eastern Province holds 41% of goats but only 9% of sheep; West and North hold 81% of sheep (AHS 2020, LVS-01). The Livestock Master Plan says goats suit every zone and sheep suit medium and high rainfall zones (LVS-09). [E] Eastern lowland sites: goats; Nyabihu: sheep (including Merino-type crosses) alongside goats.
- **Breeds.** [V] 76% of goats are local Small East African type, 7.9% crosses, 2.5% pure Boer, 0.3% Galla, 13.4% unknown (LVS-01); 97% local in Bugesera (LVS-10). Local adults weigh 20 to 25 kg (LVS-01). RAB is multiplying Merino sheep at Gishwati for approved farmers (LVS-56). [U] No Rwandan growth, kidding or mortality data for Boer crosses were found, and no Dorper programme.
- **Health.** [V] PPR was declared in September 2023 and was "still endemic" in May 2024; strategy 2024-2030 (LVS-14); PPR antibodies were already found in Bugesera animals before 2021 (LVS-45). RVF outbreaks came in 2022, 2024 (Ngoma) and 2026 (Eastern Province and parts of the North) (LVS-11, LVS-12, LVS-03). Eastern lowland parasite load is heavy: Haemonchus in 71.8% of goats and 83.4% of sheep in Nyagatare (LVS-46); worms were the top problem in Bugesera with deaths peaking in April-May (LVS-10); goat brucellosis is endemic in Nyagatare (LVS-39). No small ruminant was vaccinated in 2020 (LVS-01).
- **Rules.** [V] Moves between sectors and districts need veterinary approval via IremboGov "Livestock Relocation" (free, 3 days) (LVS-29); a web permit system (ARPMS) exists (LVS-08); the 2008 animal-health law makes owners responsible for immunisation and requires immediate reporting of suspected disease (LVS-40); slaughter only in RICA-licensed premises (LVS-28). A consolidated animal law was before Parliament in July 2026 (page status 'Plenary'); enactment is not confirmed [U], so the 2008 law is the operative text until checked (LVS-31). [V, single source] In Ngoma in 2026 farmers were told not to take cattle to market without ear tags and buyers had to buy only in designated, tagged-animal market areas; the article does not mention goats or sheep (LVS-05).
- **Fodder.** [V] Unfertilised on-farm Brachiaria plots in Bugesera gave 1.2 to 5.7 t DM/ha per 2-month cut (LVS-16); first cuts 90 days after planting gave 3.0 to 9.8 t DM/ha (LVS-15). [E] These plot-scale figures are not safe for stocking-rate maths; [A] plan on 4 to 8 t DM/ha/yr of forage for rainfed semi-arid pasture (unfertilised Napier is 2 to 10 internationally, LVS-52) until a farm trial exists. Goats eat roughly 2.5 to 3.5% of body weight as dry matter daily (LVS-26, LVS-27).
- **Dairy-goat sanity check.** [V] Goat milk is not collected or processed commercially in Rwanda (PRISM SWOT, LVS-01); dairy policy targets cattle. [E] No case for dairy goats at this stage.

- [V] AHS 2020: households sold 329,954 goats and 83,257 sheep in 2020 and ate 38,342 goats and 5,538 sheep themselves (LVS-01). [E] Sales were about 19% of the goat herd (329,954 / 1,745,806) and 20% of the sheep herd (83,257 / 420,365).
- [V] The chain is "trader dominated" with many intermediaries; slaughterhouses only provide the slaughter service and traders own the meat; farmers complain of low prices from neighbours (a goat sold for Rwf15,000 in Nyagatare) and pay about Rwf500 per animal taken to market (LVS-01).
- [V] Venue-level demand in the 2022 survey: ten small hotels and bars in Nyarugenge (Kigali) bought 44 goats a week between them, and large hotels buy by weight (one reported 300 kg a week, another 30 kg; one 50 kg lamb plus 50 kg goat). Seven bars in Bugesera bought 66 goats a week and thirteen venues in Kayonza 43 (LVS-01, annex; sums computed from the annex). Venue names and contacts are in the PDF annex and are not copied.
- [V] Meat per head is low and the sources disagree: 13.2 to 14.2 kg per person per year (MINAGRI, LVS-08) versus about 8 kg (RAB official quoted in May 2024, LVS-56). NISR consumer data quoted by a trade outlet show meat prices up 33.8% (urban) and 35.5% (rural) year on year in April 2025 (LVS-33); verify against NISR.
- [V] Substitution: when beef spiked during the 2026 outbreak many consumers moved to chicken and fish (LVS-03). No evidence either way for goat.

Treat district slaughter totals as indicative only.

### 1.5 Trade (imports and exports)

- [V] DRC takes 89% of small-ruminant exports to neighbours (BNR, 2017-2021); live goats are the main product: US$3.43M (2017), 4.22M (2018), 3.62M (2019), 1.52M (2020), 5.82M (2021) (LVS-01). Hides and skins go mainly to Kenya.
- [V] Cross-border traders' cooperatives reported monthly exports in 2022 of 2,500 goats via Rubavu, 3,000 via Rusizi and 1,500 via Nyamasheke (Rugari), plus 1,500, 1,000 and 600 sheep (LVS-01). The report's own summary says "per week", which conflicts with the monthly list; the monthly reading is used here.
- [V] The Livestock Development Strategy cites 80,505 goats exported (US$2,876,768) and 14,582 sheep (US$410,452) in the NISR Statistical Yearbook 2022 (LVS-08). [E] That is about US$36 per goat and US$28 per sheep (2,876,768 / 80,505; 410,452 / 14,582). The BNR series above (US$5.8M in 2021) and this one disagree, probably different years or definitions; neither gives an RWF rate.
- [V] Imports are negligible (US$10,505 in 2021, almost all from DRC) (LVS-01). Live exports are dominated by Western border districts; DRC buyers pay less for goat meat than Rwandan markets (LVS-01).
- [E] Cross-border demand lifts prices near the border (Rubavu and Rusizi live prices are about double other districts in 2022, LVS-01; Mukamira sheep Rwf100,000 in 2024, LVS-56); Nyabihu borders Rubavu, so highland sheep may get a premium that the eastern lowlands do not.
- Export readiness is not demonstrated: exports are informal live animals, no export abattoir appears in the sources, and DRC demand is described as unstable (LVS-01).

## 6. Production models and economics

- **Meat (fattening / finishing).** Market evidence supports it: hospitality demand, year-end peaks, shrinking supply, retail Rwf7,500+/kg. Constraint: no Rwandan finishing-cost or weight-gain data was found, so margins cannot be verified (searched: Rwanda goat fattening economics, gross margin; a Rwandan smallholder gross-margin study found covers crops only).
- **Breeding-stock sales.** Demand exists (households bought 311,870 goats in 2020; projects want improved genetics, LVS-01, LVS-08, LVS-56) but needs proven, healthy, recorded animals; the buyer base is mostly poor households with low willingness to pay. [V] In Bugesera most farms castrate male kids before 3 months for meat quality, so 62.2% borrow and 10% hire bucks, 59% of farms with breeding problems cite having no buck, and only 4% keep records (LVS-10). [E] That points to local demand for healthy, proven bucks and buck services; validate price and willingness to pay. [U] Boer buck/doe prices in Rwanda not found.
- **Combination.** Plausible, but the evidence is thin; start with meat finishing of purchased or locally bred stock while building a small recorded breeding nucleus [E].
- **Sheep in the highlands.** The only price signal above Rwf50,000 per animal is Nyabihu/DRC (Rwf100,000 for 30-35 kg, LVS-56); a Nyabihu site could test a sheep-plus-goat flock, but one farmer quote is not a market study.
- [V] Farmers say prices are low relative to land, feed, medicines and time, and that markets are far and buyers few (LVS-01); the 2013 Bugesera study found 91% of farms sell year-round to meet household costs (LVS-10).
- [V] Main risks in the sources: disease (PPR, RVF, parasites), theft ("growing robbery" targeting small ruminants, LVS-01), feed and land, dry-season water scarcity in the Eastern zone (LVS-01), DRC instability and no formal contracts (LVS-01).
- [E] A first-pass revenue range per sold animal in Bugesera is Rwf40,000 to 60,000 (medium to big, 2022, LVS-01); 2025 retail meat prices rose about 45% over 2022, but live prices are not known to have followed. The financial model should carry Rwf40,000 to 60,000 as [A] (low case) and flag it for a 2026 market check.
- **By-products.** [V] Hides and skins were the second-largest small-ruminant export (US$13.5 million over 2017-2021, mostly to Kenya and Uganda); only two local processors were found and they mainly handle cattle hides (LVS-01). Wool: a Merino can give up to 4 kg a year; one Musanze producer imports more than a tonne of wool a year from Kenya; a wool hat sold at about US$40 and a pullover at US$210 (LVS-01). No goat-milk collection or processing exists (LVS-01). [U] No price for goat or sheep manure was found (searched: Rwanda goat manure price); a manure value would be an [A] in the model.
- **Risk transfer and credit.** [V] Small ruminants are not among the species listed in MINAGRI's livestock-insurance counts (cows, poultry and pigs; LVS-08), and PRISM lists the lack of a small-ruminant insurance scheme and of suitable loan products as weaknesses and threats (LVS-01).
- [V] Taxation: income from agriculture below Rwf12 million a year is zero-rated under Law 016/2018 art. 21 as cited by PRISM (LVS-01); verify in the regulatory stage.

## 9. Pasture and fodder for small ruminants

### 9.1 What Rwanda's own data show

- [V] Rwandan work is concentrated on Brachiaria: 13 improved cultivars trialled in two agro-ecologies; promoted in 20 of 30 districts, reaching over 4,800 farmers; farmers prefer it for low rainfall, acidic soils and year-round green leaf (LVS-17). - [V] Bugesera is the reference semi-arid site: 650 to 900 mm, about 1,400 m, a 4 to 5 month dry season (LVS-15). ### 9.3 Hay, silage and dry-season feeding

- [V] Dry-season feed is the binding constraint in the East (LVS-15, LVS-16, LVS-01), and the 2026 dry spell scorched pasture in the Eastern Province (LVS-13).
- [V] The Livestock Master Plan and PRISM recommend timely harvest and hay conservation, over-sowing grazing land with improved grass and legumes, crop-residue use and hydroponic fodder for land-short farms (LVS-09, LVS-01).
- [V] Chloris gayana makes good hay cut at or just before flowering, up to six cuts of 25-50 days (LVS-18); Setaria suits hay and silage (LVS-24). RAB baled 58 t of Chloris and Brachiaria hay in FY2019/20 and sold some to private farmers (LVS-50).
- [V] Conservation practice in the East is taught and used mainly for dairy cattle: RAB showed bag silage, pit silage and hay making from sorghum, maize, bean and rice straws, desmodium and calliandra leaves in Kayonza (LVS-64); a Kayonza dairy farm conserves fodder as hay, silage and sealed storage (100 of a 300 t target stored) and uses hydroponic maize fodder (LVS-62); RAB reports hydroponic fodder uses about ten times less water and takes 6-7 days, and plans to raise fodder land from 32,000 to 87,000 ha (LVS-63).
- [U] Rwandan hay or silage practice for goats and sheep, with costs, prices per bale and losses, was not found.

### 9.4 Dry-matter intake and a stocking illustration

- [V] Ruminants eat about 2.5 to 3% of body weight as dry matter per day (LVS-26); goats can eat up to 6.5% (LVS-27); late-pregnant needs about double and lactating about triple late-summer maintenance (LVS-26).
- [V] Drinking water at maintenance is 2 to 3 times dry matter intake (about 1.5 to 3.3 L per goat per day) and 4 to 5 times when lactating (LVS-27). [E] 100 adult goats at maintenance need roughly 150 to 330 L a day, more in the dry season and with kids; this matters for Bugesera, where dry-season water is scarce (LVS-01).
- [V] Water infrastructure cost example: a 160 m solar-powered borehole with a 1.3 km pipeline cost Rwf80 million at an 18 ha dairy farm in Gahini, Kayonza, with a Rwf49 million subsidy; the area once went two years without rain (LVS-62). Treat as an upper reference for dairy scale, not a goat-farm quote.
- [E] Method: daily DM = body weight x 2.5 to 3.5%.

| Body weight | Daily DM at 2.5% | Daily DM at 3.5% | Annual DM at 3% |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20 kg (young/local small) | 0.5 kg | 0.7 kg | 219 kg |
| 25 kg (local adult) | 0.6 kg | 0.9 kg | 274 kg |
| 40 kg (Boer cross, [A]) | 1.0 kg | 1.4 kg | 438 kg |
| 60 kg ([A]) | 1.5 kg | 2.1 kg | 657 kg |

- [E] Illustration: if usable forage is 4 to 8 t DM/ha/yr [A] and 50% of it is actually eaten [A] (the rest is trampled, rejected or left for regrowth), one hectare gives 2,000 to 4,000 kg of eaten DM. That carries 7 to 15 local adult goats (2,000 / 274 to 4,000 / 274) or 5 to 9 animals of 40 kg (2,000 / 438 to 4,000 / 438). Lactating does with kids need roughly twice the intake, which halves these numbers. [A] Validate with a one-season grazing trial and fortnightly pasture cuts.

## Open questions and field validation

1. Exact location (pin or UPI) and zone: elevation, rainfall, dry-season length, water source (lake use may be restricted), neighbouring RVF/PPR history.
2. Is the Eastern quarantine lifted, and are Bugesera, Rwamagana, Kayonza and Ngoma markets open? Ask RAB and the district veterinary officer; ask what ear-tag and movement rules apply now and whether a disease-free farm may sell direct to abattoirs.

4. Who in Rwanda sells healthy improved breeding stock (Boer/Galla crosses, Merino crosses): RAB Nyagatare and Gishwati, private breeders, projects; prices, health status, records.
5. Performance and mortality of improved genotypes on farm (kidding interval, litter size, growth, losses) in Rwandan conditions; local vet advice on parasite control, brucellosis testing and the vaccination calendar.
