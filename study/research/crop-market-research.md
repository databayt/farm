# Crop and tree market research

Status: Stage 1 desk research — preliminary
Researched: 2026-10-19
Location dependency: High. The farm's location is unconfirmed (candidate zones: Lake Rumira/Gashora in Bugesera, a drier eastern lowland; Lake Muhazi shore east of Kigali; Lake Mugesera area; Shyira in Nyabihu, western highlands). Suitability, programme eligibility and haulage to Kigali differ by zone, and each crop section and section 5 say where. No site-specific claim is made.

## At a glance

- **Avocado is the best-evidenced fruit line; the market is real but young** [V CRP-01]. NAEB reports exports rising from about 840 t (USD 840,500) in 2019/20 to over 4,201 t (over USD 8.6 m) in 2024/25, from 9,654 farmers on 549,074 trees, about 88% of them aged 0-6 years (Hass 73%, Fuerte 27%). A grower-exporter claims a 10,000 t season in 2025-26 [V CRP-19; not reconciled with NAEB]. Risks: about 90% of exports go to one destination (UAE) by air [V CRP-01], and the global market was oversupplied in 2025-26 with start-of-season prices about 20% lower [V CRP-19].
- **A farmer is paid far less than the export value.** FAO's national producer price for avocados is RWF 573/kg in 2024 (RWF 284/kg in 2020) [V CRP-16] against an average export value of about USD 2.05/kg, roughly RWF 3,000/kg at the assumed RWF 1,480/USD [E CRP-P26]. A small farm earns the first figure unless it sells into an out-grower or export programme (inference [E]). One Huye farmer reported RWF 120 per fruit in January 2024 [V CRP-02].
- **Rwandan yield-by-age data are not published; plan with ranges.** An irrigated commercial exporter reports 12-15 t/ha now and 17-20 t/ha at maturity on about 400 trees/ha [V CRP-20]. An Australian irrigated benchmark ramps from 12 kg/tree in year 3 to 45-51 kg/tree from year 6-7 [V CRP-45, international, optimistic]. For rain-fed smallholder planning this study uses 50% of that ramp [A, validate with RAB or a mature local orchard].
- **Oranges, lemons and mangoes are imported in volume** [V CRP-18]: in 2024 oranges 5,882 t, lemons and limes 1,920 t and mangoes/guavas 2,904 t (7,808 t in 2023); imported Kenyan mangoes cost RWF 900-1,000 each at supplier level in Kigali in April 2026 [V CRP-10]. Local mango farm-gate was RWF 500-600/kg in an August 2025 farmer report [V CRP-09] but RWF 1,394/kg in FAO's 2024 group price [V CRP-16]: a 2.3-2.8x gap to resolve in the field.
- **Subsidy and programme levers could change the budget.** NAEB's PSAC project (with IFAD) hands free grafted avocado (150,000), mango (45,000) and macadamia (10,000) seedlings to beneficiary farmers in seven districts including Bugesera and Rwamagana in the 2026A season [V CRP-12]; the fertiliser subsidy to 31 Dec 2026 covers fruit trees (urea at most RWF 1,090/kg against RWF 1,980 retail) [V CRP-46]; the SSIT irrigation subsidy pays 50% or 75% of list price [V CRP-35]. Eligibility of a privately owned 2-10 ha farm is [U].
- **Passion fruit and tree tomato are highland crops with serious constraints.** A 2026 survey found woodiness-disease symptoms in 81% (Rusizi) to 100% (Rwamagana) of fields and Ugandan passiflora virus in all seven districts tested [V CRP-33]; NAEB lists the main passion fruit zones as northern highland districts [V CRP-34]. Tree tomato needs 15-20 C and tolerates neither frost nor drought [V CRP-34], so it fits Shyira, not the eastern lowland [E].
- **Macadamia, banana and pineapple are low-value fits.** Macadamia farm-gate RWF 1,100-1,400/kg in 2025 (RWF 2,000 in 2019), nuts after about 5 years [V CRP-08]; cooking banana RWF 387/kg producer price and endemic Xanthomonas wilt [V CRP-16, CRP-53]; pineapple RWF 458/kg producer price and 3.9 t/ha national yield [V CRP-16, CRP-17]. Chilli is the short-cycle cash crop with the clearest export-chain evidence, but farm-gate reports conflict (RWF 600/kg minimum vs RWF 2,500/kg) [V CRP-26, CRP-27].
- **Timber is regulated and thinly rewarded.** The 2024 forest law needs a licence to harvest trees on any area (previously from 2 ha) and bans cutting, moving or trading poles under 20 cm diameter without a special permit; IremboGov lists a harvest permit (30 days, free) and a transport permit (2 days, free) [V CRP-06, CRP-03, CRP-04]. Grevillea earned a benefit-cost ratio of 1.06 for Musanze farmers in 2022 [V CRP-37], and policy discourages eucalyptus near water [V CRP-41, 2006 brief via a 2016 thesis]. Treat trees as boundary, shade and pole assets, not a revenue line, at this budget [E].
- **Zones differ.** Bugesera/Gashora is a PSAC district for avocado and mango [V CRP-12, CRP-13], hosts a KOICA/NAEB chilli post-harvest centre [V CRP-12] and is described as drought-prone [D CRP-66]; Lake Mugesera, in the Ngoma/Kirehe part of the Eastern Province [A], lies in NAEB's pineapple and mango zones [V CRP-34]; the Muhazi shore is at about 1,443 m [D CRP-64]; Shyira (Nyabihu) is highland, where tree tomato, passion fruit, Alnus and Grevillea fit and mango and pineapple do not [E].
- **Not found** (searched NAEB, RAB, RFA, FAOSTAT, WFP, press, journals): a Rwandan nursery price list or certified-nursery registry, a current Hass farm-gate series, current farm-gate prices for passion fruit, tree tomato, guava and papaya, pole and timber prices, and NAEB's avocado export-instruction thresholds.

## 1. How to read this file

- **Labels**: [V] verified, with the source ID; [E] estimate, method shown; [A] assumption, to be validated; [U] unknown. Every CRP-nn ID is a row in `study/registers/incoming/crp-sources.csv`; price rows are CRP-Pnn in `crp-prices.csv`; supplier rows are CRP-Snn in `crp-suppliers.csv`.
- **Price types are kept apart**: farm-gate/producer, supplier (wholesale), retail, export unit value. A press quote from one farmer is [V] only as "a farmer reported", not as a market price.
- **Currency**: RWF. USD only with a rate. **[A] USD 1 = RWF 1,480**, the rate implied by the founder's "USD 25k is about RWF 37 m" (2026-10-19). The BNR reference rate could not be retrieved (bnr.rw returned no rate); replace it when the financial model is built.
- **Age of evidence**: sources older than about 5 years are flagged. FAOSTAT and WFP series are used for order of magnitude and seasonality, not as quotes. WFP fruit prices stop in December 2015, except bananas (to July 2026).
- **International vs Rwandan**: Rwandan sources come first; where a Rwandan figure was not found, an international one is used, named as international, and never presented as a Rwandan expectation (the Australian avocado yield ramp is the main case).
- **Access limits**: some sites blocked automated reading (allAfrica, MDPI, CIFOR-ICRAF PDFs, KALRO, rfa.gov.rw, the Rwanda FDA list). Where only a search-engine summary was seen, the claim is not used as [V]. The New Times article bodies were read through their `/amp` pages; journal abstracts through Crossref records.
- **Two "RICA"**: the Rwanda Inspectorate, Competition and Consumer Protection Authority (rica.gov.rw; seed and phytosanitary licensing) is not the Rwanda Institute for Conservation Agriculture (Bugesera).

**Cultivars available in Rwanda**: NAEB lists Hass, Fuerte, Ettinger, Booth, Choquette, Collinson, Nabal, Puebla, Reed, Simmonds, Pinkerton and Bacon, with Hass and Fuerte the export types [V CRP-34]; Souk Farms' last season was 90% Hass, 5% Fuerte, 5% Pinkerton [V CRP-19]. **Grafted vs seedling**: grafted plants only [A]; the Eastern Province study found 86.5% non-improved trees [V CRP-32]. **Spacing and plants per ha**: 6-12 m squares give 280-69 trees/ha (international) [V CRP-44]; Souk Farms plants about 400/ha (16,800 trees on 42 ha) [E from CRP-20]; the Australian table implies about 310-330/ha [E from CRP-45]. For a rain-fed mixed farm with livestock, 8 x 8 m (156/ha) is a sensible working assumption [A]. **Pollination**: avocado cultivars flower in two complementary types; Hass is normally treated as type A and Fuerte as type B and an orchard mixes both [A: ask the nursery for the flowering type of each cultivar; CRP-44 describes the mechanism but does not classify cultivars].

### 2.6 Guava and papaya

- **Guava**: Kigali has only one retail observation (RWF 700/kg, 2015); the all-market median was about RWF 200/kg in 2012-15 [V CRP-11, older than 5 years]. FAOSTAT has no separate guava line (it is combined with mango). No Rwandan demand, buyer or agronomy evidence found [U]. Not recommended for planning.
- **Papaya**: FAO producer price RWF 708/kg (2024; RWF 358 in 2020); national output only 134 t from 15 ha harvested in 2024 [V CRP-16, CRP-17]; Kigali retail 2015 median RWF 693/kg (Bugesera RWF 500/kg), with a flat seasonal price index [V CRP-11, older than 5 years]. FAOSTAT shows negligible commercial output; whether the cause is demand, disease or data gaps is unknown, and no Rwandan agronomy or buyer evidence was found [U]. Treat as a garden/ancillary fruit.

### 3.3 Markets, prices and rotations

- **Poles, timber and fuelwood prices**: not found for 2024-2026 (searched: RFA, New Times, NISR, WFP, CIFOR-ICRAF/GIZ 2019 "Wood Supply Chain in Rwanda" - the PDF was blocked). The only price series found is WFP retail charcoal: Kigali median RWF 6,974 per sack in 2015 [V CRP-P25, older than 5 years].
- **Value check**: Grevillea's 1.06 benefit-cost ratio [V CRP-37] and the thesis finding that farmers keep eucalyptus for wood and cash despite crop suppression [V CRP-41] both indicate that wood is a low-margin, long-horizon asset. With under USD 25k and fruit and livestock to establish, plant boundary and shade rows only (for a square 5 ha block the perimeter is about 894 m [E], so about 300 trees at 3 m spacing), buy seedlings from RFA or Tubura, and defer any felling until the permit route above is confirmed [E].

### 4.3 What this means for the budget (illustration, not a plan)

A 3 ha orchard of 1.5 ha avocado (8 x 8 m), 1 ha mango and 0.5 ha citrus would cost about RWF 1.93 m + 1.19 m + 1.03 m = **RWF 4.1 m (USD 2,800)** to year 3 with guards and before irrigation [E]. Adding irrigation at RWF 1.5-3.5 m/ha gives **RWF 8.6-14.6 m (USD 5,800-9,900)**, which is 23-39% of the RWF 37 m available for years 1-2, before fencing, water works, livestock and working capital [E]. Irrigation and fencing, not trees, set the pace; PSAC's free seedlings and the 50-75% SSIT and fertiliser subsidies are the levers to confirm first [V CRP-12, CRP-35, CRP-46].

### 4.4 Shortlist for the Stage 3 decision matrix [E]

Carry forward: grafted avocado (staged, with a buyer route), grafted mango (drier zones), irrigated chilli under a written buyer agreement (Bugesera), a small citrus trial (lemon first), and boundary trees only. Deprioritise: macadamia, banana as a cash crop, passion fruit (unless certified planting material and a buyer exist), guava, papaya, and tree tomato outside highland sites.

## Open questions and field validation

The five that could change the investment decision:

1. **Price truth**: what is a Hass grade-1 kilogram worth at the farm gate near the site, and what does a mango, lemon or chilli kilogram fetch at Kimironko/Nyabugogo and at the farm gate? Resolve the conflicts (mango RWF 500-600/kg vs RWF 1,394/kg; chilli RWF 600/kg vs RWF 2,500/kg). Method: a structured market price survey with at least three buyers per crop (no outreach has been done).
2. **Subsidy eligibility**: can a privately owned or company-held 2-10 ha farm receive PSAC or IFAD seedlings, the fertiliser subsidy (Smart Nkunganire needs ID and parcel number) and the SSIT irrigation subsidy? Needs the district agronomist and the land-title UPI.
3. **Rwandan yield by age**: a measured record of grafted Hass and mango yields per tree and year from a RAB station, a PSAC orchard or a mature private orchard; replace the 50% planning haircut.
4. **Planting material**: written quotes (price, cultivar, rootstock, mother-tree source, delivery) from at least three named nurseries for grafted Hass, Fuerte, mango and lemon, and an answer on whether certified, virus-tested passion fruit seedlings exist.
5. **Water and site**: lake or borehole access rights, rainfall and frost record, soil pH and drainage, because avocado tolerates no waterlogging and mango needs a dry spell at flowering [V CRP-34].

Also open:

6. Obtain NAEB's "Instructions for exporting avocados from Rwanda" (dry matter, size, packing) and the exporter-licence steps.
7. Forest law: Official Gazette number, whether fruit trees need a harvest licence, and how districts issue permits in practice.
8. Tree-protection rule for goats and sheep: no tropical or East African source was found; use trial blocks and ask RAB or an animal-science adviser for a site rule.
9. Processors and aggregators: verify current buying of mango, pineapple, passion fruit and citrus (Inyange, Urwibutso, Rwamagana banana centre, dried-pineapple makers) and their payment terms; 2011-2021 reports show payment and sorting disputes.
10. Not reviewed: coffee economics for the higher zones, strawberries, dragon fruit and jackfruit; current pole, timber and charcoal prices; citrus greening and fruit-fly status in the site district.
