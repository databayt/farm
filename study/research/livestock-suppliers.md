# Livestock, veterinary and pasture-seed sources

Status: Stage 1 desk research — preliminary  
Researched: 2026-10-19  
Location dependency: High. Which stations, markets, vets and seed routes are practical depends on the farm's district (candidate zones: Bugesera, Ngoma, Rwamagana/Kayonza/Gicumbi/Gatsibo/Gasabo on the Muhazi side, and Nyabihu) and on the RVF movement rules in force when the farm buys. The location is unconfirmed [U].

Evidence labels: [V] verified (source ID), [E] estimate, [A] assumption, [U] unknown. A listed supplier is not confirmed stock. Nobody was contacted. Contact details are given only where an official or business source publishes them (one entry: the veterinary council's WOAH listing); private individuals' mobile numbers printed in public reports are deliberately not copied. Full records are in `lvs-suppliers.csv`; sources in `lvs-sources.csv`.

## At a glance

- **No private breeder with public stock and prices was found.** [U] Searched: Boer goat farm Rwanda, ihene za Boer, Rwanda commercial goat farm, Rwanda goat farmers cooperative; results were Ugandan, Zimbabwean or generic. PRISM's annex names 25 large goat and sheep farmers with mobile numbers (2022) but that is a list of people, not an offer (LVS-01). AHS 2020 found only 22 large-scale goat farmers (100+ animals) in the country (LVS-01).
- **Institutional routes exist but are undocumented for the public.** [V] RAB Nyagatare station is listed as providing improved goat genetic material and fodder seed (LVS-01) and, with its Karama farm and Mirama feedlot, grows fodder, forage seed and germplasm; it baled 58 t of hay in FY2019/20 and sold some to private farmers (LVS-50). RAB Gishwati multiplies Merino sheep for approved farmers (LVS-56). [U] No stock list, price or contact was found for any of these.
- **Livestock markets are the volume source, but were largely shut in the East in 2026.** [V] Bugesera, Rwamagana and Kayonza markets were closed on 2026-08-21; Ngoma, Nyagatare, Gatsibo and Kirehe markets reopened on scheduled days with cattle ear tags and designated buying areas (LVS-05, single source). Nyabihu has a weekly market at Mukamira with DRC sheep buyers (LVS-56). Market animals have unknown origin and health; PPR arrived in 2023 apparently with about 300 goats moved informally (LVS-14).
- **Biosecurity first.** [V] PPR is endemic (LVS-14), RVF outbreaks came in 2022, 2024 and 2026 (LVS-03, LVS-11, LVS-12), brucellosis is endemic in Nyagatare goats (LVS-39), Haemonchus infects about three quarters of Nyagatare small ruminants (LVS-46), and parasites kill Bugesera goats mainly in April-May (LVS-10). The qualification method below makes origin, health records and quarantine the buying criteria before price.
- **Vets exist; a public directory does not.** [V] The Rwanda Council of Veterinary Doctors has about 6,000 members, 1,748 of them practising in 34 veterinary companies (LVS-04); there are 15 proximity veterinary posts and 5 laboratories (LVS-08); RAB sells vaccines to farmers and vets through IremboGov (LVS-44). [U] Vet pharmacies and agrovets near the candidate zones: not found with published addresses. Four Kigali veterinary wholesalers appear in an excerpt of the Rwanda FDA licence list, unverified (LVS-48).
- **Pasture seed.** [V] RAB sells forage seed through an IremboGov service ("Purchase the Forage Seed": price by type and amount, 7 days, pick-up at a RAB station; the page speaks of veterinarians and asks for an RCVD licence number) (LVS-60). [U] No Rwandan seed company with a published forage price list was found. RAB is the only documented multiplier (stations: Nyagatare, Rubona, Songa, Kinigi/Musanze, Gishwati) (LVS-50, LVS-51); Bugesera nurseries distributed Calliandra and Gliricidia seedlings in 2019 (LVS-50). Foreign vendor sheets exist but are not a Rwandan source.
- **Abattoirs and meat traders** are sale routes, not animal suppliers. [V] Saban (Nyabugogo) is Kigali's main slaughter service for goats and sheep; Rugari Meat Processing had 700 goats at Gako (Bugesera) in 2016 (LVS-54).

## 5. Supplier qualification method

Applies to any source of animals: RAB station, breeder, cooperative, trader or market. Steps 1 to 3 are desk and phone checks to be done by the founder or an authorised person; nobody has been contacted for this study. Every veterinary item is **to be confirmed with a Rwandan veterinarian**; the thresholds below are [A] placeholders, not medical advice.

1. **Specification before shopping.** Write the purchase specification: species and breed type, sex and age, target live weight, number, health status, ear-tag requirement, delivery date. [A] Validate with the vet and with Kigali buyers' preferred weights.
2. **Screen the origin.** Record district and sector of origin. Reject animals from districts under quarantine or with recent RVF, PPR or other notifiable-disease reports on the purchase date (check RAB or the district veterinary office). Prefer a single closed herd to dealers and market animals; PPR reached Rwanda with animals of unknown origin (LVS-14). Note that Eastern lowland origins carry higher parasite and brucellosis risk (LVS-46, LVS-39).
3. **Legal paperwork.** Require the IremboGov livestock-relocation certificate (sector approval within a sector, district approval between districts) and, where the origin is an infected, suspected or protected zone, the veterinary movement permit required by Law 54/2008 arts. 18-19 (LVS-29, LVS-40). Keep copies. Check the seller's identity and whether the animals are tagged (Ngoma required ear tags for cattle at markets in 2026, LVS-05; goats and sheep were not mentioned, but tag the farm's own animals anyway [A]).
4. **Health inspection at origin, by the farm's own vet.** Visual check of every animal (body condition, anaemia signs, coat and skin lesions, eyes, nose and mouth lesions, cough or nasal discharge, diarrhoea, lameness and hoof condition, udders in does, testicles in bucks). PRISM's buck/ram selection criteria add: strong legs and hooves, good libido, two well-developed testicles, free of transmissible and parasitic disease, and avoid genetically polled breeding males (LVS-01). The vet decides what else to test.
5. **Records to ask for.** Birth dates, dam and sire, litter size and kidding or lambing history, deworming and tick-control dates and products (written, not verbal), vaccination dates for RVF and PPR and any other vaccines, treatments, abortions and deaths in the flock, and brucellosis results for breeding animals (seroprevalence of 6% to 11% in Nyagatare goats, 16.6% of herds, LVS-39). No records means a lower score and a quarantine-only purchase.
6. **Transport.** Licensed vehicle, cleaned before loading, one source per load, no mixing with market animals, correct documents in the cab, no movement during quarantine, plan around any night-travel restriction that traders reported (LVS-01), water and shade, minimum journey time.
7. **Quarantine on arrival.** Keep new animals apart from the resident flock, with separate water and pasture and no contact with cattle (mixing cattle and goat herds was the strongest brucellosis risk factor, LVS-39). The length of quarantine, tests and treatments are set by the farm's vet; this file does not prescribe them. Record daily observations.
8. **Acceptance and payment.** Pay in stages: deposit, balance after the vet's release at origin, final release after quarantine; weigh at purchase; ear-tag and register each animal on arrival; keep the right to reject sick animals.
9. **Score and monitor.** Score each supplier 0 to 5 on origin clarity, documents, health history, genetics records, price, delivery reliability and response; re-score after 6 and 12 months using the farm's own mortality, growth and fertility records. A supplier with unexplained deaths in the first 3 months is dropped.
10. **Red flags.** Unknown origin, no tags or records, animals offered cheaply from a quarantined district, refusal of vet inspection, sellers who will not provide a relocation certificate, animals from a flock with recent abortions.

## Open questions and field validation

1. Does RAB release breeding goats, rams, forage seed, hay or planting material from Nyagatare, Gishwati, Kinigi or other stations to private farms, at what price and with what health certification?
2. Which private farms, cooperatives or projects in or near Bugesera, Ngoma, Rwamagana, Kayonza and Nyabihu hold recorded improved stock; are there any with published stock lists? Does Rugari/Gako still hold goats?
3. Which vet clinics and veterinary pharmacies serve the farm's sector; fees for visits, vaccination, testing and the cost of the RVF/PPR vaccines when not free.
4. Whether the RICA-licensed abattoirs near the farm (Gako, Rwamagana, Kayonza, Kigali) accept direct supply from farms and what they pay.
5. The seed and planting-material suppliers and prices (Brachiaria, Chloris, Setaria, Desmodium, Napier, Calliandra); the species, stations and prices behind RAB's IremboGov forage-seed service, and whether a farm may apply without a vet.
6. The current quarantine and market status in each district on the purchase date.
