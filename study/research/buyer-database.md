# Kigali buyer landscape and prospect list

Status: Stage 1 desk research — preliminary
Researched: 2026-10-19
Location dependency: none (Kigali market)

## At a glance

- **Goat and sheep supply shock, current.** [V BUY-28, BUY-29, BUY-09] RAB quarantined cattle, goats and sheep from 2026-06-26 (six districts, then all Eastern Province and parts of Northern Province). By 2026-08-13 slaughter and meat sales were banned in affected areas, livestock markets were closed, bars and restaurants there could not serve meat from affected livestock, and in July the Nyabugogo abattoir had gone four days without goats or sheep and was drawing mainly on the Western and Southern provinces. Four Eastern districts reopened livestock markets on 12 days between 2026-08-21 and 2026-09-28 (ear tags required for cattle in Ngoma); markets in Bugesera, Rwamagana and Kayonza stayed closed. The position after 2026-09-28 is [U]. Healthy, traceable goats near Kigali could be valuable, but movement and market rules can shut the live-animal channel without notice [A, validate with the district veterinary officer]. If the farm lies in the Eastern Province (several lakes near Kigali, for example in Bugesera, are there), the quarantine would apply to its own animals [A; location unconfirmed].
- **Kigali goat demand is real, trader-controlled and the meat price has risen.** [V BUY-10] In 2022 the Nyabugogo abattoir said it slaughtered at least 120 goats and 40 sheep a day, the chain was "trader dominated", farmers had no buyer contracts, and goat meat sold at RWF5,500/kg in Kigali. The same report's MINAGRI table records only 9,309 goats and 3,375 sheep for Nyarugenge District in 2021 (about 25 goats a day), four to five times lower than the abattoir's figure [E: 120 / (9,309 / 365)], so Kigali goat volumes are uncertain. [V BUY-30] In mid-2025 a vendor quote was RWF7,500/kg for goat and sheep meat, up from RWF6,000. The two figures differ in method and market, so the rise of about 36% is indicative only [E: (7,500 - 5,500) / 5,500]. Hotels and bars named as goat or mutton buyers in the 2022 RAB annex: M Hotel 300 kg, Ubumwe Grande 50 kg lamb and 50 kg goat, Onomo 30 kg, bars 2-10 goats a week; the period behind the kilogram figures is not stated [V BUY-10].
- **Fruit: several buyers say they buy from farmers, and exporters recruit outgrowers.** [V BUY-03, BUY-37, BUY-41] Afri-Farmers Market (five Kigali outlets; a supplying farmer says it collects, weighs and pays immediately), KabFresh (trains outgrowers, sells fruit in Kigali) and Pride Farms (says at least 50% of purchase value goes to local growers) are self-reported. Garden Fresh invites outgrowers and cites 600 farmers [V BUY-55]; BAHAGE (3,000+), ABCD Great Life (375+) and S&I Fresh (250) describe outgrower networks [V BUY-35, BUY-34, BUY-06]. No source publishes a minimum volume, grade, price or payment term for outgrowers [U]. Kigali stalls also sold imported fruit in April 2026 (mangoes from Kenya, watermelons from Tanzania and Uganda, apples from South Africa), and fruit prices rose after fuel-price increases that doubled the transport cost of a sack from RWF1,000 to 2,000 [V BUY-59].
- **The exporter market is crowded in avocado.** [V BUY-31] NAEB lists 69 fruit and vegetable exporters; 38 list avocado, 17 passion fruit and one tamarillo (counted by this stream from the page table). [V BUY-05] The exporters' association vice-president says Rwanda will compete in "premium markets in limited volumes", and 16 members are pursuing GlobalG.A.P. A 2-10 ha farm is at most an outgrower; export potential is not export readiness.
- **Supermarkets: three names, no published terms, and a hint of unmet local fresh-produce supply.** [V BUY-01, BUY-02, BUY-33] Simba reports 15 branches; SPAR opened with Sawa Citi in April 2025; a U Express at Inzovu Mall was announced for early 2026 and no opening report was found. None publishes supplier requirements, volumes or payment terms [U]. Simba's deputy manager said not all needs, "especially when it comes to fresh produce", can be met locally [V BUY-56; secondhand]. Whether that signals unmet demand or quality and consistency gaps is untested [A].
- **No central wholesale market in years 1-2.** [V BUY-22] The Kigali Wholesale Market for Fresh Produce was announced in 2010, was due in 2018 and is unbuilt; a January 2026 report puts it at about EUR47.5m and 265,000 t a year [V BUY-23, excerpt only]. No opening date is published [U]. Plan around existing markets, retailers, hotels and aggregators.
- **Public procurement is a poor early channel; one NGO buyer looks more approachable.** [V BUY-19, BUY-20, BUY-21] Umucyo notices seen in October 2026 use national competitive bidding, bid validity of 120 days, a tender fee of 10,000 (currency not stated) and tender security, and the first listing page showed no fruit, goat or sheep supply tender. Solid'Africa buys from smallholders for hospital and school meals (self-reported, 6,000+ farmers) but its pages do not mention fruit or meat [V BUY-40].
- **Hotels are reachable through official lists.** [V BUY-11] A UN document prepared in July 2026 lists 34 UNDSS-cleared Kigali hotels; 14 are in the prospect list with the business email as published there. Hotel sourcing statements are generic ("finest locally sourced ingredients" [V BUY-12]); only Heaven names suppliers [V BUY-16].
- **What was not found.** The prices any Kigali buyer pays farmers and their order sizes (only one retailer's shelf prices were captured [V BUY-63]); payment terms for any buyer; fruit or goat purchasing by schools, universities, hospitals or prisons; independent caterers that publish sourcing; the Rwanda FDA licensed-business lists (indexed by search but returning HTTP 404). [U]
- **Quality of evidence.** The register holds 53 prospects. Most facts rest on one media article, one company page or one 2022 survey, and several company sites carry 2021-2023 footers. No price, volume or payment term has been confirmed by a buyer. The field kit in section 4 exists to close that gap.

*Reading notes.* Labels: [V] verified (source ID), [E] estimate (method shown), [A] assumption (validation named), [U] unknown. Every ID is a row in `study/registers/incoming/buy-sources.csv`; the full prospect rows are in `study/registers/incoming/buy-buyers.csv`. Nobody was contacted for this work. Every entry is a prospect, not a customer: no interest, relationship or agreement is implied, and a listed business is not confirmed demand. USD amounts are the sources' own figures; the BNR reference rate on 2026-10-19 was RWF 1,475.24 per USD [V BUY-50], so US$1 million is about RWF 1.48 billion [E: 1,000,000 x 1,475.24].

### 1.1 Produce wholesalers and market traders

- **What it buys.** Fruit and vegetables for resale; meat traders work in the same markets, and journalists quoted retail beef at Nyarugenge Market (boneless RWF11,500-12,000/kg) and Kimironko Market (boneless RWF10,000/kg) in July 2026 [V BUY-09; retail price, vendor-quoted, observed July 2026]. Fruit lines and volumes by market were not captured in this pass [U].
- **Quality and packaging.** No published specification [U]. Expect mixed grade in bulk crates or sacks with price as the main test [A; validate with the price sheet in 4.2 and trader interviews].
- **Order size and frequency.** Not found (searched: New Times, KT Press, FreshPlaza and Kilimo Trust coverage of Kigali markets and wholesale chains; Kinyarwanda and French queries) [U].
- **Direct from farms.** Unknown [U].
- **Market conditions.** In April 2026 a Gisimenti fruit vendor reported mangoes imported from Kenya at RWF900-1,000 each, oranges at RWF1,200/kg, and suppliers blaming higher transport costs after the 2026-04-04 fuel-price rise [V BUY-59; retail, one hub].
- **Direct-to-consumer venue.** The Kigali Farmers' and Artisans' Market ran on the first Saturday of each month at Kigali Serena Hotel from 2018 [V BUY-27; 2019 source]; a current schedule was not found [U].
- **Delivery.** Farm delivers at market opening to the trader's stall or a dropping point [A]; market fees and entry rules not checked [U].
- **Payment.** Not public [U]; cash on delivery is the working assumption [A; validate in interviews].
- **Approach.** Two weekday-morning visits with the price sheet, then a first trial lot to the two or three most reliable traders.
- **Evidence to qualify.** Three traders each quote price and volume for the same product in two consecutive weeks and agree cash on delivery for a first lot.
- **Planned wholesale market.** The Kigali Wholesale Market for Fresh Produce (Kigali Special Economic Zone, Gasabo) is unbuilt, has slipped since 2010 and carries no published opening date [V BUY-22, BUY-23; excerpt]. It is not a year 1-2 channel [E: no opening date found as of 2026-10-19].

### 1.2 Supermarkets

- **What it buys.** General groceries including fresh produce. Simba says it stocks "locally sourced products" and cites "direct sourcing, partnerships with suppliers" without naming items [V BUY-01]. SPAR Rwanda, run by Sawa Citi since April 2025, calls itself "premium yet local" [V BUY-02]. A French cooperative-retail entrant (U Express at Inzovu Mall, Kimihurura) said it would work with local suppliers; it was planned for early 2026 and no opening was found [V BUY-33; U on opening].
- **Quality and packaging.** Not published for any chain [U]. In 2012 buyers at Nakumatt and Frulep said local suppliers failed on consistent supply, supply delays and nutrition labelling [V BUY-26; 14 years old, shows what buyers complained about, not current terms]. Expect graded, clean, labelled, packed produce for modern retail [A; validate with a category buyer].
- **Order size and frequency.** Not found (searched: supplier-registration pages and news for Simba and Sawa Citi) [U].
- **Direct from farms.** Unknown [U]. Simba's wording hints at some direct sourcing but names no farm or cooperative [V BUY-01]; its deputy manager said local supply of fresh produce is still developing [V BUY-56; secondhand].
- **Delivery.** Store-door or central receiving, scheduled windows, invoice and delivery note [A].
- **Payment.** Not public [U]. Supermarket credit periods of 30-60 days are a regional norm [A; validate; no Rwandan source found].
- **Approach.** Ask the store manager or head-office category buyer for the supplier-onboarding process; bring a priced sample box and the farm's licences once production exists.
- **Evidence to qualify.** A written trial order or listing confirmation stating product, grade, packaging, price per kg, weekly volume and payment days.
- **Landscape check (which chains operate).** Simba reports 15 branches nationally in August 2026 [V BUY-01], against 13 in a September 2025 summary [V BUY-62]; Sawa Citi converted a Nyarutarama store to SPAR in April 2025 and is moving its stores to the SPAR format [V BUY-02, BUY-62]; the same 2025 summary names the centrally located 2000 Supermarket and Sharma Supermarket (imported specialty goods) as other local operators and does not name Nakumatt or Uchumi [V BUY-62; inference E that they are no longer major operators]. Sawa Citi store locations (Kimihurura, Remera, Kimironko) come from a 2021 consumer guide and are unconfirmed [BUY-25, grade D].

### 1.3 Online grocers and delivery platforms

- **What it buys.** Afri-Farmers Market sells fruit, vegetables and meat products in stores and online with home delivery and supplies hotels, restaurants and schools [V BUY-03, BUY-44]. Pride Farms sells produce, pantry goods and dairy with free delivery and says at least 50% of purchase value goes to local growers [V BUY-41]. Vuba Vuba and Isokko are delivery marketplaces rather than direct buyers [V BUY-42, BUY-43].
- **Quality and packaging.** "Quality and consistency" is claimed but no specification is published [V BUY-03; U for detail].
- **Order size and frequency.** Not published [U]. Afri-Farmers collects from farms and weighs on collection [V BUY-03].
- **Direct from farms.** Afri-Farmers: yes, per a supplying farmer quoted in the article [V BUY-03; self-reported]. Pride Farms: self-reported [V BUY-41]. Platforms: no [V BUY-42, BUY-43].
- **Delivery.** Operator collects from the farm (Afri-Farmers) [V BUY-03]; collection days and minimum quantity unknown [U].
- **Payment.** Immediate payment to a supplying farmer [V BUY-03; one testimony]. Customer subscriptions run on 3-, 7- and 15-day terms [V BUY-03].
- **Approach.** Ask for the supplier-onboarding steps: product list, grading, collection route, price basis and payment day. For platforms, ask how a seller becomes a partner merchant and what commission applies [U].
- **Evidence to qualify.** Written price list or trial collection order with a named product, kg and price.
- **Caution.** Pride Farms and KabFresh show 2021-2022 footers; their 2025-2026 trading is not confirmed [U; BUY-41, BUY-37].

### 1.4 Hotels

- **What it buys.** Food and beverage for restaurants, banquets and room service. Goat or mutton is bought by some: RAB 2022 Annex 1 records M Hotel 300 kg, Ubumwe Grande 50 kg lamb and 50 kg goat, Onomo 30 kg, and lists Marriott and Serena without quantities [V BUY-10; period not stated]. M Hotel's restaurant describes Mediterranean, Levantine and Lebanese-inspired dishes and its breakfast uses "fresh local produce" [V BUY-52]. Fruit items are not published [U].
- **Quality and packaging.** Chef-driven: consistent size and ripeness, clean, labelled, cold chain for meat [A; validate by chef interviews]. Hotels state "locally sourced" intent but publish no specification [V BUY-12, BUY-16]. A 2018 trade-press report says high-end hotels complained about "poor quality" of local fruit and vegetables and import instead [V BUY-57; unattributed and eight years old; no import-share study found].
- **Order size and frequency.** Not published beyond the 2022 kilogram figures [U].
- **Direct from farms.** Unknown for all [U]. Heaven names German Butcher, Kigali Farms, Hope Haven and GET IT as local suppliers and runs an onsite garden [V BUY-16]. Kigali Serena hosted a monthly farmers' market from 2018 [V BUY-27].
- **Delivery.** Early-morning back-of-house delivery with invoice and delivery note [A].
- **Payment.** Not public [U]; 30 days is a common hotel norm [A; validate].
- **Approach.** Use the hotel's published contact or switchboard to reach the executive chef or purchasing manager; offer a tasting lot; do not name relationships.
- **Evidence to qualify.** Chef sign-off on a sample against a written spec, a first order with price, kg and payment days, and a named receiving contact.
- **Source of leads.** 34 hotels appear in a UN list of UNDSS-cleared Kigali hotels prepared in July 2026 [V BUY-11]; the Rwanda Hospitality Association member directory was in maintenance (HTTP 503) on 2026-10-19 [V BUY-24].

### 1.5 Restaurants and brochette bars

- **What it buys.** Goat for brochettes: bars in Nyarugenge reported 2-10 goats a week each in 2022, selling brochettes at RWF700-1,500; across Kigali brochettes ranged RWF800-3,000 and one goat produced RWF60,000-360,000 of turnover [V BUY-10; 2022]. Restaurants with cuisines that use mutton (for example the Indian restaurant Khana Khazana, in business since 2005 per its site) may buy lamb or goat, but no menu or volume was found [V BUY-47; A on demand].
- **Quality and packaging.** Likely dressed, chilled goat by carcass or cuts; fat cover and tenderness drive repeat buying [A; validate].
- **Order size and frequency.** Weekly, small [V BUY-10; 2022].
- **Direct from farms.** Unknown [U]; the chain is trader-dominated [V BUY-10].
- **Delivery.** Dressed carcass delivered cold and early [A].
- **Payment.** Likely cash or short credit [A; validate].
- **Approach.** In-person visits to owner-managers; offer two or three goats a week at an agreed price per kg for four weeks.
- **Evidence to qualify.** Owner states weekly goats or kg, price per kg and payment day, and takes the trial lot.
- **Caveat.** The six bars in the register come from a 2022 survey; their 2025-2026 status is not confirmed. In July-August 2026 restaurants in quarantined areas could not serve meat from affected livestock [V BUY-28]. In March 2026 restaurant operators said input costs were rising and quickly passed into menu prices, and a market worker said goat and chicken had become more expensive [V BUY-58]; thin margins may make price-stable local supply attractive but also make slow payment risky [A].

### 1.6 Fruit distributors and aggregators

- **What it buys.** Fruit and vegetables from outgrowers and farmers for retail, hotels, export and processing. Examples: KabFresh (passion fruit, avocado, mango; sells fruit in Kigali; trains outgrowers) [V BUY-37]; Afri-Farmers Market [V BUY-03]; GET IT, named as a hotel and restaurant supplier by Heaven [V BUY-16] but with a parked old domain, an unreadable possible new one (getit.africa, blocked to automated fetch) and no 2024-2025 trading evidence [U]; and the exporters with local lines (SOUK Farms says it also serves the Rwandan market [V BUY-36]).
- **Quality and packaging.** Specifications are not published [U]. BAHAGE markets "safe, hygienic" produce available year-round [V BUY-08].
- **Order size and frequency.** Not published [U].
- **Direct from farms.** Yes by their own statements [V BUY-03, BUY-37, BUY-55].
- **Delivery.** Collection from farm or aggregation point [V BUY-03 for Afri-Farmers; A for others].
- **Payment.** Immediate payment is claimed by one farmer for Afri-Farmers [V BUY-03]; others unknown [U].
- **Approach.** Ask which crops and grades they need, minimum volumes, collection points and payment day.
- **Evidence to qualify.** Written offtake or collection schedule.

### 1.7 Processors (juice, pulp, wine, jam, dried fruit)

- **What it buys.** Inyange Industries sells juices and nectars including passion, mango, pineapple, orange and apple, from its Masaka plant in Kigali [V BUY-38]; in 2012 it procured about 160 t of pineapple and 90 t of passion fruit a month [V BUY-39; 14 years old]. ZIZA sa Ltd (Kimironko) is listed by NAEB as making pineapple juice, wine, biscuits and jam [V BUY-31; no second source]. Urwibutso Enterprise (Rulindo) makes juices and wine and is said to buy crops from about 3,000 contracted farming families [V BUY-48; grade D].
- **Quality and packaging.** Not published [U]. In 2012 Inyange's chairman said farmers' produce quality was a problem [V BUY-39]. Processors usually accept lower cosmetic grades but need sound, ripe fruit in volume [A; validate].
- **Order size and frequency.** 2012 volumes above are the only figures [V BUY-39]; current volumes [U].
- **Direct from farms.** Historically yes for Inyange (farmer-supplied pineapple and passion fruit [V BUY-39]); current practice [U].
- **Delivery.** Delivery to the plant or a collection point [A].
- **Payment.** Not public [U].
- **Approach.** Ask each processor how it sources fresh fruit today, minimum lot, price basis and payment day; expect volumes above this farm's capacity and a role as surplus or second-grade outlet [A].
- **Evidence to qualify.** A written intake price and lot size for a named fruit.
- **Gap.** Rwanda FDA manufacturer lists (March and June 2026) would name more processors but returned HTTP 404 [U].

### 1.8 Exporters

- **What it buys.** NAEB lists 69 exporters of fruit and fresh vegetables, 38 with avocado and 17 with passion fruit [V BUY-31; counted by this stream]. Company pages show Garden Fresh (30 t a week; SMETA, BRCGS and Global GAP certificates; 600 farmers, company-stated) [V BUY-55], BAHAGE (targets 50 t a week by air and 23 t a week by sea; GlobalG.A.P. for avocados and French beans) [V BUY-35], ABCD Great Life (375+ smallholders; avocado 192,000 kg and passion fruit 96,000 kg a year, company-stated) [V BUY-34] and SOUK Farms (over 200 ha, exports to the UK, UAE and EU) [V BUY-36].
- **Quality and packaging.** Export-grade grading and certification; 16 association members are pursuing GlobalG.A.P. [V BUY-05]. Detailed specifications [U].
- **Order size and frequency.** Company-level scale is large [V BUY-55, BUY-35]; outgrower minimums are not published [U].
- **Direct from farms.** Outgrower networks are claimed [V BUY-55, BUY-35, BUY-34, BUY-06; self-reported].
- **Delivery.** To packhouse or collection point, with traceability records [A].
- **Payment.** Not public [U].
- **Approach.** Use the outgrower routes some publish (Garden Fresh, ABCD Great Life, BAHAGE): ask whether small outgrowers are taken, what volume, which varieties, harvest-maturity tests and price basis.
- **Evidence to qualify.** Written outgrower terms and a first-season purchase programme. Export potential is not export readiness.

### 1.9 Meat traders, butcheries and abattoirs

- **What it buys.** Cattle, goats and sheep from farmers and markets; abattoirs provide slaughter service only and return meat to traders, who sell to retailers [V BUY-10].
- **Quality and packaging.** Inspection and slaughter at a licensed abattoir; carcass cold chain for hotels [A; validate with RICA and the abattoir]. Moving live goats between sectors or districts calls for a livestock relocation certificate from the sector or district veterinary authority, applied for on Irembo, free, about 3 days [V BUY-60]; whether it is issued for quarantined areas is [U]. Businesses that slaughter, transport or sell unprocessed meat must register with RICA before using premises, slaughter only in designated areas, and meat handlers need six-monthly medical certificates [V BUY-61; search excerpt only, verify with RICA].
- **Order size and frequency.** The Nyabugogo abattoir reported at least 120 goats and 40 sheep a day in 2022, but the report's MINAGRI table shows 9,309 goats and 3,375 sheep slaughtered in Nyarugenge District in 2021 and 1,170 and 78 in Gasabo (Kicukiro is absent) [V BUY-10; the two disagree by four to five times, so treat Kigali volume as uncertain]. Nationally the table shows 161,502 goats in 2021; its 143,490 sheep for 2021 includes one Gatsibo entry of 113,685 that looks like an error, leaving about 29,805 [E: 143,490 - 113,685]. Paniel's wholesale buyers order two, five or ten tonnes [V BUY-17; 2021].
- **Direct from farms.** Traders buy from farmers and markets [V BUY-10]; Paniel works through contract farmers [V BUY-17; self-reported].
- **Delivery.** Live delivery to market or abattoir, or dressed carcass delivery [A].
- **Payment.** Not public [U].
- **Approach.** Visit Saban abattoir and two or three butchers to learn the live-weight and carcass price basis and who buys dressed goat.
- **Evidence to qualify.** A trader or butcher states price per kg live or dressed, weekly head count and payment day, and takes a trial lot.
- **Supply risk.** The 2026 quarantine interrupted goat and sheep deliveries to Nyabugogo and banned slaughter and meat sales in affected areas [V BUY-09, BUY-28].

### 1.10 Institutional buyers (schools, universities, hospitals, prisons, public tenders)

- **What it buys.** Solid'Africa delivers meals to patients in public hospitals and students in public schools and says it sources from 6,000+ smallholder farmers (cumulative, self-reported); fruit and meat are not mentioned [V BUY-40]. Public bodies buy through Umucyo: catering (WASAC Development Ltd, published 2026-10-08) and livestock (Rwamagana District cows, 2026-10-09) were open in October 2026 [V BUY-19, BUY-20]. University, hospital and prison fruit or goat purchasing was not found [U].
- **Quality and packaging.** Tender specifications [U].
- **Order size and frequency.** Not stated in the mirrored notices [U].
- **Direct from farms.** Open tenders are for registered firms and consortia [V BUY-20]; Solid'Africa says it buys from smallholders [V BUY-40; self-reported].
- **Delivery.** Delivery to a named site, with a delivery note [A].
- **Payment.** Not stated [U].
- **Approach.** For tenders, defer until the farm has a company registration, tax and social-security clearances and a production history, and learn the Umucyo registration steps first [U]. For Solid'Africa, ask whether fruit is on its menus and how it contracts farms.
- **Evidence to qualify.** An open tender whose lot size and delivery location fit the farm, with a fee and security the farm can afford (observed: fee 10,000, currency not stated; tender security required; bid validity 120 days [V BUY-19, BUY-20]); or a written purchase arrangement from Solid'Africa.

## 4. Field validation kit

These are tools, not evidence. Thresholds marked [A] are working assumptions to be replaced by what buyers say. Record every answer verbatim, tag it "buyer-stated" and label it [A] until a written order or price list exists.

### 4.1 Buyer interview guide (30-40 minutes, in person, Kinyarwanda, French or English)

Before starting: say who the farm is, that this is market research, that nothing is being sold today, and ask permission to take notes.

**A. Business and role**
1. What do you buy for the business? Who decides what to buy and who approves a new supplier?
2. How many outlets or covers do you serve? Which days are busiest?

**B. Products, varieties and volumes**
3. Which fruit do you buy (avocado, mango, passion fruit, tree tomato, papaya, citrus, banana, others)? Which varieties and sizes? (For goat and sheep: live or dressed, cuts, age, weight, sex.)
4. How much per week or month in kg, pieces or head? Does it change by season, holiday or event?
5. How often do you order, and on which days?

**C. Quality specifications and packaging**
6. What size, ripeness, colour, brix or shelf-life do you require? What do you reject? (For goat and sheep: live weight, fat cover, health papers, slaughter certificate, halal.)
7. How must it be packed (crate, carton, net, bag, labelled)? Do you return or reuse crates?
8. Do you ask for a licence, inspection or health certificate? Which ones? Do you need proof of movement permits or ear tags?

**D. Delivery**
9. Where, when and how do you take delivery? Who unloads and checks? What lead time and minimum order do you need?
10. What happens when a delivery is late, short or rejected?

**E. Prices**
11. What did you pay last week for each item, per kg or piece or head? Is that farm-gate, wholesale or delivered? How do you set the price and how much does it swing across the year?
12. Would you pay more for a better grade, for reliable weekly delivery or for a specific variety?

**F. Payment terms**
13. How do you pay (cash, mobile money, bank, cheque)? After how many days? Do you need an invoice or a tax receipt?
14. Have you been paid late by anyone or paid suppliers late? What is your longest delay?

**G. Current suppliers and their problems**
15. Who supplies you now (farmers, cooperatives, traders, aggregators)? How many?
16. What goes wrong with them (quality, quantity, price, delay, trust, disease restrictions)? Which problem costs you most?

**H. Trial and commitment**
17. Would you try a sample lot from a new farm? What size lot and when?
18. If quality and price are right, how many kg or head a week could you commit to, for how many months?
19. What would make you stop buying from us?
20. May we follow up, and who else should we speak to (a chef, a driver, a trader, a competitor)?

Rules for the collector:
- Take at least three prices per product per market, from different sellers, at the same time of day, two market days a week for four weeks.
- Never mix units. Convert to RWF per kg only in analysis, and keep the raw unit.
- Record RVF or other movement restrictions on the day [V BUY-28 and BUY-09 show they change prices and supply].
- Quote USD only with the BNR reference rate and its date (RWF 1,475.24 per USD on 2026-10-19 [V BUY-50]).
- Cross-check against the national market information system (e-Soko, cited as the source of market prices in [V BUY-09]) and note any gap of more than 15% [A].

### 4.3 Qualification criteria

A prospect is **qualified** only when all "must" items are evidenced in writing or by a recorded interview, and at least four of the six "should" items.

**Must**
1. Operating in the last 12 months, with a named decision-maker role.
2. Buys a product the farm can grow or raise, in a weekly volume the farm can meet (set the minimum and maximum volume once the product plan exists) [A].
3. States a price and its basis (farm-gate, wholesale, delivered).
4. States payment terms of 30 days or less, or accepts a deposit or security [A].
5. Accepts the farm's delivery route, packaging and grading.
6. Has no unresolved compliance blocker (licence, inspection, movement permit).
7. Agrees to a trial lot.

**Should**
1. Pays by mobile money or bank with invoice.
2. Buys year-round or across a window the farm can cover.
3. Will sign a letter of intent.
4. Gives a reference (a current supplier we may call).
5. Is within a day's delivery of the farm.
6. Buys more than one product from the farm's list.

**Disqualify** when: required volume is far above capacity; payment is over 60 days with no security; the buyer demands exclusivity; a certificate is required that cannot be obtained in 12 months; the buyer cannot say who pays; or the buyer refuses a written order after two trials.

**Status ladder:** prospect, interviewed, qualified, trial lot delivered, letter of intent signed, repeat order. Only the last two count as demand in the financial model, and only at the volume written down.

### 4.4 Letter of intent or preliminary purchase commitment (outline)

Non-binding except for the clauses marked binding. Have a Rwandan lawyer review before use [A].

1. **Parties.** Buyer and farm legal names, registration numbers, authorised signatories, addresses.
2. **Purpose.** A non-binding statement that the buyer intends to purchase the products below once the farm begins supply.
3. **Products and specification.** Species, variety, grade, size, ripeness or live weight, packaging and labelling, as an annex.
4. **Indicative volume and schedule.** Start date, weekly or monthly range, seasonal windows, ramp-up plan.
5. **Price.** Fixed price, formula, or reference to a published market price with floor and ceiling; review dates; currency RWF.
6. **Quality inspection and rejection.** Who inspects, when, rejection criteria, replacement or credit.
7. **Delivery.** Place, days, time window, who unloads, delivery notes, cold chain where needed.
8. **Payment.** Days from delivery, method, invoice and receipt requirements, late-payment consequences.
9. **Trial period.** Number of weeks, trial volumes, and agreed review meeting.
10. **Compliance.** Licences, health and inspection papers, movement permits (including disease restrictions), traceability records each party must keep.
11. **Force majeure and disease.** Weather, quarantine, road closures: volumes suspended without penalty; notice period.
12. **Term and termination.** Duration, notice to end, non-exclusive.
13. **Confidentiality** (binding) and **governing law and dispute route** (binding).
14. **Non-binding statement.** This letter records intent only and does not oblige either party to buy or sell until a supply agreement or purchase order is signed.
15. **Signatures and date;** annexes: specification sheet, price table, delivery schedule.
