# Plant, seed and input suppliers

Status: Stage 1 desk research — preliminary
Researched: 2026-10-19
Location dependency: Medium. Supplier distance, delivery cost and programme eligibility depend on the district (candidate zones: Lake Rumira/Gashora in Bugesera, Lake Muhazi shore, Lake Mugesera area, Shyira in Nyabihu). A listed supplier is not confirmed stock; no supplier, buyer or office was contacted.

## At a glance

- **Verified planting-material channels are programmes, not price lists.** One Acre Fund-Tubura is described as arguably the largest avocado seedling supplier (450,000 Hass and Fuerte seedlings in 2022); in October 2021 it distributed 384,000 avocado trees in 27 districts, 67,500 free and 316,500 subsidised and paid in instalments [V CRP-21, CRP-22]. It reports a network of over 2,000 community nurseries and opened a 1.4 ha Kigali nursery (target over 1 million seedlings a year) in July 2026 [V CRP-48, CRP-24]. See CRP-S01.
- **NAEB's PSAC project gives free grafted seedlings to beneficiary farmers** in 13 districts; the 2026A season put 150,000 avocado, 45,000 mango and 10,000 macadamia seedlings into seven districts including Bugesera and Rwamagana, produced by PSAC-supported women and youth groups whose names are not published [V CRP-12, CRP-13]. Eligibility of a privately owned farm is [U]. See CRP-S04.
- **Published seedling prices are scarce.** Local nurseries sold avocado seedlings at RWF 800-1,500 each and imported ones at about USD 4.5 (about RWF 5,000), per a January 2024 press report with no nursery named [V CRP-02, CRP-P32]. No price was found for grafted mango, citrus, macadamia or passion fruit seedlings.
- **No certified-nursery registry was found** (searched NAEB, RAB, RFA/Irembo, the seed regulator RICA, press). The only private nursery found is a directory listing with no public contact and no price [V CRP-25]. The qualification checklist in section 6 is therefore a good-practice list, not a Rwandan accreditation scheme [A].
- **Forestry seed and seedlings** are ordered from the Rwanda Forestry Authority through IremboGov (5 days; price by quantity; collect at a chosen centre) [V CRP-05]; the National Tree Seed Centre is in Huye and, in 2020, farmers recalled that almost 90% of its seed had failed before its restoration [V CRP-54].
- **Irrigation**: all pumps are imported; the SSIT scheme pays 50% or 75% of list price through approved retailers (13 named for 2020) and farmers pay the rest up front [V CRP-35]. Davis & Shirtliff publishes five Rwanda branches with telephone numbers [V CRP-49]. See CRP-S06 to CRP-S13.
- **Inputs**: the fertiliser subsidy to 31 December 2026 covers fruit trees (urea at most RWF 1,090/kg to the farmer) and runs through licensed agro-dealers and the Smart Nkunganire registration [V CRP-46]; a seed merchant needs a RICA licence (RWF 150,000, 14 days) [V CRP-51].

## 3. Forestry seed and seedlings

- **Rwanda Forestry Authority (CRP-S03)**: orders go through IremboGov under "Infrastructure and Environment"; the applicant needs a national ID (or passport) and a TIN if an organisation; processing is 5 days; the price depends on the quantity of each seed type; payment is online with a billing number starting 88; pick-up is at the collection centre chosen on the form [V CRP-05]. The page lists no species, no prices and no collection centres.
- **National Tree Seed Centre, Huye**: centralises tree-seed supply nationally; moved from RAB to the forestry agency in 2014; the Ruhande Arboretum holds 207 exotic and indigenous species; in 2020, 30% of the centre's seed came from the arboretum and 70% from outside (nine agroforestry cooperatives and imports from Kenya); farmers recalled that almost 90% of its seed had earlier failed to grow, and an interviewee said quality improved after IUCN support [V CRP-54, older than 5 years]. Ask for germination test results before buying.
- **One Acre Fund-Tubura (CRP-S01)** supplies Grevillea and other agroforestry seedlings and reports 2,000+ community nurseries [V CRP-48].
- **How many seeds a boundary needs** [E]: about 300 trees round a 5 ha square block; at 60-80% germination for Grevillea [V CRP-38] that is about 430 seeds, i.e. 4-18 g at 24,000-105,000 seeds/kg; Maesopsis seed loses viability quickly (700-1,000 seeds/kg) [V CRP-40]; Alnus has about 2 million seeds/kg [V CRP-55]. Buying 300 seedlings is simpler than raising them [A].
- Seedling prices for forest species: not found (searched: RFA, Irembo, press; a Ugandan price list exists but is not Rwandan).

## 4. Seed companies, agro-dealers, irrigation and other inputs

**Seed regulation and sellers.** Anyone selling seed as retailer, importer, exporter or distributor needs a Seed Merchant Licence from RICA (the Rwanda Inspectorate, Competition and Consumer Protection Authority): RWF 150,000, 14 days, TIN, storage-facility lease or ownership, proof of seed certification and a staff contract; RICA inspects the site [V CRP-51]. Whether grafted fruit seedlings are covered is not stated [U]. A seed centre opened in Bugesera on 6 October 2023 at the Rwanda Institute for Conservation Agriculture (a different institution with the same acronym) serves seed companies (maize, potato, multigrain), not fruit trees or the public [V CRP-52]. Pasture and fodder seed suppliers are covered by the livestock/forage workstream.

**Agro-dealers and fertiliser** (CRP-S14). For the 2027 Season A (1 July-31 December 2026) urea retails at RWF 1,980/kg and the farmer pays at most RWF 1,090/kg; subsidies are 45% urea, 46% DAP, 43% NPK 17:17:17, 18-46% other blends; eligible crops include fruit trees, bananas and vegetables; farmers register in the Smart Nkunganire System (national ID and land parcel number) by phone, through sector agronomists or at registration centres; licensed agro-dealers place orders through MOPA, and each delivery is verified by the agro-dealer, the Agro-Processing Trust Corporation, the supplier and the sector agriculture officer [V CRP-46, CRP-P24]. No individual dealer was identified; the sector agronomist is the route to the nearest licensed dealer. Whether a company-owned farm can register is [U].

**Irrigation and pumping** (CRP-S06 to CRP-S13).

- All irrigation pumps are imported (many via Dubai); common brands are Dayliff (Davis & Shirtliff), Honda, Koshin and Vlais; manufacturing in Rwanda is limited to pipes [V CRP-35].
- The Small Scale Irrigation Technology (SSIT) scheme sells subsidised equipment only through approved retailers; the government pays 50% or 75% of the list price and the farmer pays the rest up front; the retailer posts a RWF 5,000,000 guarantee, and transport, installation and repair are not covered by the subsidy [V CRP-35, April 2022]. A 2016 report also gives the subsidy as 50% [V CRP-58, older than 5 years].
- SSIT list prices: treadle pump RWF 90,000; petrol pump RWF 87,500-300,000 plus suction and hose pipes RWF 83,500-150,000; drip and sprinkler systems are "expensive" and used on 1-10 ha farms, typically donor- or project-funded [V CRP-35, CRP-P34].
- Retailers approved for SSIT in 2020: ETC Agro Tractors & Implements, Pro Water Rwanda Ltd, INTERTECH, Innovatechs Ltd, Enterprise de Construction Mixte, Water Energy Center, Holland Greentech Ltd, Ignite Power Rwanda Ltd, Open Construction & Related Services Ltd, Nikki Production Ltd, General Consultancy & Trading Company Ltd, Overseas Logistics General Services Ltd and SOCOSE Ltd; V&D General Trading Ltd is a non-SSIT distributor [V CRP-35]. Their current status is unconfirmed.
- Davis & Shirtliff (CRP-S06) publishes five Rwanda branches (Kigali Muhima, Remera, Gakiriro, Rubavu, Rusizi) with telephone numbers; it lists pumps, solar and irrigation kits [V CRP-49]. Holland Greentech (CRP-S07) distributes Rivulis drip irrigation and greenhouses [V CRP-50].
- Gabiro scheme water is billed at RWF 277/m3 (about USD 0.19) and irrigation of maize there costs about RWF 1.8 m/ha; this state scheme is not comparable with pumping from a lake or borehole [V CRP-57, CRP-P35]. Lake abstraction needs legal access and is covered by the water-rights workstream.

**Processors and aggregators that buy fruit are not suppliers**, but they matter for sourcing choices: Inyange (pineapple, 2011), the Rwamagana Banana Community Processing Centre (target 65 t of bananas a day, 2021), Norelga Macadamia Rwanda (macadamia), Tuzamurane (dried pineapple), and exporter Souk Farms' out-grower scheme [V CRP-63, CRP-43, CRP-08, CRP-62, CRP-20]; current buying is unverified and they belong in the buyer register. Two further juice makers (one in Bugesera) were seen only in a search-engine summary of Rwanda FDA licence lists that could not be opened; they are not recorded.

## 6. How to qualify a nursery (checklist) [A]

No Rwandan accreditation scheme for fruit nurseries was found, so this is a good-practice list to be checked with a RAB agronomist before any purchase. Do not rely on a listing, a photograph or a promise of stock.

1. **Registration**: company registration and TIN; any licence the nursery claims (a seed merchant licence from RICA applies to seed sellers [V CRP-51]; whether grafted seedlings need one is [U]); a physical site you can visit.
2. **Plant-health documentation**: a written inspection or health record from RAB or the district agronomist [U: the issuing body for nurseries was not found]; no leaf spots, galls, wilting or root rot; clean potting medium; for passion fruit, virus-free planting material, a nursery isolated from older vines, and sanitised tools, because woodiness virus is widespread and the authors urge certification [V CRP-33].
3. **Mother-tree source**: the named orchard that supplies budwood, cultivar identity (Hass, Fuerte, Pinkerton; Tommy Atkins, Kent, Apple, Zillate) and the date of selection; a photo and location of the mother trees; no budwood from unknown fruit.
4. **Rootstock and grafting quality**: rootstock type and seed source; a fully healed union with no gap or girdling wire; union clear of the soil; straight, single leader; no root coiling in the bag; age and height stated; hardened off for several weeks before sale.
5. **Traceability**: each batch labelled with cultivar, graft date and mother source; written invoice listing cultivar and quantity.
6. **Replacement policy**: written, with a stated window (e.g. 3 months) for failed grafts or wrong cultivar.
7. **Price, minimum order, delivery**: quote in writing; delivery in shade and watered; plant within days; no advance payment above a small deposit [A].
8. **References**: visit two customers whose orchards are 2-3 years old; check cultivar identity, survival and growth.
9. **Programme seedlings (PSAC, Tubura, IFAD)**: ask which nursery produced them and the cultivar mix, because the programmes do not publish nursery names [V CRP-13].
