# Preliminary research report — Stage 1

Status: Stage 1 synthesis · Date: 2026-10-19 · Location: **unconfirmed**. Nothing here is a site-specific feasibility study.
Inputs: five desk-research streams (location, markets and institutions, crops, livestock, buyers): 306 sources, 329 price observations, 35 supplier and 53 buyer leads. All are in `../registers/`, and every cited ID resolves.

## 1. The answer so far

1. **The farm can't be designed until it is located.** "Kuroshira" is not in Rwanda's list of 14,837 villages, sectors and cells. Ranked by sound, nearness to a lake and closeness to Kigali, the best matches are:
   1. **Lake Rumira at Gashora/Rilima, Bugesera** (about 58 km, 50 min from Kigali);
   2. **Gishari on Lake Muhazi's south shore, Rwamagana** (about 53 km);
   3. **Mugesera**;
   4. **Shyira, Nyabihu** (110 km; a highland zone, quite different) [E LOC-17, LOC-18, LOC-19].
2. **The lake is a constraint, not a resource, until proven otherwise.** Verified in the legal text:
   - Land within 50 m of the furthest flood line is State public domain [V LOC-01].
   - No farm or livestock installation may stand within 50 m of a lake bank, and no kraal or livestock market within **200 m** [V LOC-02, Law 48/2018 Art 42, re-checked against the gazette text 2026-10-19]. The penalty is RWF 500,000 plus removal (Art 49).
   - Irrigation over 1 ha, and any pump in a lake, needs a Rwanda Water Resources Board permit: RWF 35,000, a decision within 90 days, valid up to 15 years [V LOC-04].
   - In the Bugesera zone the small lakes store little water and are ringed by declared swamps [V LOC-46, LOC-20].
3. **Land tenure matters more than expected.** Under the 2021 land law, freehold is for Rwandans. A foreign national or foreign-owned company gets an emphyteutic lease of up to 99 years, or a concession of up to 49 years, for investment purposes only [V MKT-07, LOC-07]. Agricultural land is leased for 99 years, and a foreign holder must apply to renew [V LOC-45]. The founder said the land is "mine or my company's"; the exact title type and its term decide whether long-cycle trees are a safe investment.
4. **There are current, sourced prices.** MINAGRI's e-Soko has data to 2026-10-14. National 30-day medians, farm-gate / wholesale / retail, RWF per kg [E from V records, MKT-03]:

   | Product | Farm-gate | Wholesale | Retail |
   |---|---:|---:|---:|
   | Avocado | 600 | 700 | 800 |
   | Passion fruit | 1,800 | 2,000 | 2,400 |
   | Mango | 1,100 | — | — |
   | Tree tomato | 1,300 | 1,500 | — |
   | Papaya | 700 | 750 | — |
   | Goat meat | 7,250 | 8,000 | 8,500 |

   Bugesera markets pay less for avocado (510) and Kigali markets more (700).
5. **Livestock faces a live disease shock.** A Rift Valley fever quarantine on cattle, goats and sheep has covered the whole Eastern Province since 2026-06-26, which includes every eastern candidate zone. Livestock markets there were still closed in mid-August, and the Nyabugogo abattoir went days without goats [V LVS-03, LVS-05, MKT-09, BUY-28]. Some districts reopened markets between 21 August and 28 September [V BUY-29]. Current status is unknown [U].
6. **Goats beat sheep in the eastern zones.** In Bugesera, about 20 times more households keep goats than sheep [V LOC-24]. Goat meat is 19% of national meat output and sheep 5% [V LVS-02]. Retail goat meat rose from RWF 6,000/kg (2024) to RWF 7,500–8,000/kg (mid-2025) [V LVS-02]. Worms (Haemonchus) are the top health problem [V LVS-10, LVS-46].
7. **Avocado is the strongest tree-crop story, but it is slow and thin at farm-gate prices.**
   - Exports grew from about 840 t to more than 4,200 t between 2019/20 and 2024/25, and NAEB is planting avocado in Bugesera and Rwamagana [V CRP-01, LOC-23].
   - No Rwandan yield-by-age data exist. The planning case is half an Australian irrigated benchmark, about 24 kg per tree at maturity [A CRP-45].
   - An irrigated Rwandan exporter reports 12–15 t/ha [V CRP-20]. The gap between those two figures is the single biggest uncertainty in the study.
8. **Passion fruit is the fast-cash candidate,** at the highest farm-gate price per kg among the fruits. It carries woodiness virus, which is present in all districts tested [V CRP-33], and needs trellis capital.
9. **Buyers exist, but most fruit and goats move through traders.**
   - NAEB lists 69 exporters (38 for avocado, 17 for passion fruit) [V BUY-31]. Several run outgrower schemes.
   - Afri-Farmers Market collects from farms and pays on the spot [V BUY-03, one farmer's account].
   - Simba says it cannot source all its fresh produce locally [V BUY-01].
   - No farmer contracts were found in the goat chain [V BUY-10].
   - All 53 buyer leads are **prospects only**.
10. **Public support is real but partial.**
    - NAEB's PSAC project gave free grafted seedlings in Bugesera and Rwamagana, but its grant window closed on 2026-02-27 [V CRP-12, MKT-40].
    - Fertiliser subsidies cover fruit trees [V CRP-46], and the SSIT irrigation subsidy pays 50–75% [V CRP-35].
    - The national agricultural insurance scheme covers neither goats, sheep nor fruit trees [V MKT-21].
    - BDF merged into BRD on 2025-12-31 [V MKT-35].
    - Whether a privately owned or foreign-held farm qualifies for any of these is unknown [U].

## 2. What the financial model says, with today's evidence

The model (`../feasibility/financial-model.xlsx`) now uses sourced prices, the BNR rate (RWF 1,475.24/USD on 2026-10-19 [V MKT-04]) and the researched avocado ramp. **Costs are still placeholders [A]**, so these results are directional only.

Base case, 5 ha, capital RWF 35M:

| Farm model | Investment, 10 yrs | Peak funding need | Funding gap | EBITDA positive | 10-yr NPV (real, 12%) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|---:|
| M1 conservative mixed | 22.2M | 41.8M | 6.8M | not within 10 yrs | −32.7M |
| M2 diversified commercial | 41.3M | 42.6M | 7.6M | year 2, then erodes | −30.5M |
| M3 tech-enabled integrated | 73.3M | 87.8M | 52.8M | year 2 | −73.7M |
| M1, downside | 30.1M | 95.0M | 60.0M | never | −59.1M |
| M1, upside | 17.0M | 22.6M | 0 | year 3 | +0.7M (IRR 10.7%) |

**What this means [E]:**
- On 5 ha at Rwandan farm-gate prices, the fixed overheads (about RWF 3M a year for a worker, admin, utilities, maintenance) are as large as the revenue a mature 1.25 ha orchard and a 40-doe herd produce.
- Viability will come from four levers:
  - higher-value lines (passion fruit, irrigated avocado yields closer to the exporter benchmark);
  - selling above farm-gate (direct to Kigali buyers or exporters);
  - a lean cost base;
  - possibly more land.
- Technology spending (M3) deepens the gap unless it lifts yields or prices measurably.
- The overheads and capital costs are unverified. Getting real quotes is the most valuable next step for the model.

## 4. What depends on the location, and what doesn't

- **Location-independent (usable now):**
  - Kigali prices and buyers;
  - the institutions and permits;
  - the land-law rules for foreign holders;
  - disease risks;
  - the supplier leads;
  - the financial model's structure.
- **Location-dependent (blocked):**
  - crop and cultivar choice (altitude, rainfall);
  - water source and permit;
  - usable area after the buffers;
  - where the house and kraal can go;
  - soil correction costs;
  - transport cost to Kigali;
  - the RVF status for that district.
