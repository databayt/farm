# Crop management

Status: Stage 7 draft · Created: 2026-10-19 · Location: **unconfirmed**
The crop mix is not chosen: it waits for the site (G1), buyers (G2) and the Stage 3 matrix. Sections below apply **if the crop is selected**. Rates, intervals and spray products are not given here; they come from the sector agronomist and the lab results (T07, T13). Evidence labels as in `CLAUDE.md`. Calendar: `../execution/five-year-roadmap.md` section 2.

## 1. Principles for every crop

1. **Grafted or certified material only.** Fruit trees are grafted [A]; the Eastern Province study found 86.5% non-improved avocado trees [V CRP-32]. Passion fruit vines come from virus-indexed or certified stock because woodiness and Ugandan passiflora virus were found in every district tested [V CRP-33].
2. **Plant at the start of the rains.** Season B (March–May) is the main window; NAEB gives March–June for avocado [V CRP-34]. Every planting date is logged against the rain gauge.
3. **Drainage first.** Phytophthora root rot is the most severe avocado disease on poorly drained soil [V CRP-44]. No tree goes in a wet spot, a hollow or the lake buffer.
4. **Mulch and water before fertiliser.** Young trees fail from drought and weeds before they fail from missing nutrients.
5. **Livestock stay out of young orchards** until the trunks are guarded or the block is fenced (zoning concept rules 1–3).
6. **Records per block and per tree** (`recordkeeping.md`, `Plantings`, `CropOps`, `Harvest`).
7. **Chemicals only on the agronomist's written advice, only products registered for the crop in Rwanda, with the label's pre-harvest interval respected** [U: the registration authority and the product list to be confirmed with the sector agronomist].
8. **Lake first.** Nothing is sprayed, spread or stored within the buffer; no runoff to the shore. No installation within 50 m of the lake bank [V LOC-02].

## 2. The yearly crop cycle

| Window | Orchard | Fodder and pasture |
|---|---|---|
| Season A rains (Oct–Dec) | Gap-fill, weeding, mulch top-up, pest scouting, light pruning after harvest; avocado supply runs about September–March [V CRP-19] | Re-sow gaps, first cuts, hay if dry spells allow |
| Short dry (Jan–Feb) | Holes, compost, soil correction for new blocks; planting-material orders | Cut for hay if growth allows; repair paddocks |
| Season B rains (Mar–May) | **Planting**; staking; guards; establishment watering if dry spells; fertiliser as advised | **Sowing**; first cut about 90 days after sowing [V LVS-15] |
| Long dry (Jun–Aug) | **Watering rota**; thick mulch; shade for new transplants; fruit-fly watch | Dry-season reserve in use; no grazing of scorched pasture [V LVS-13] |
| Sep | Land preparation; pruning where the crop allows | Plan sowing and cuts |

## 4. Watering

| Item | Practice |
|---|---|
| Source | Stored rainwater first; groundwater or lake only under the permit rules: irrigation over 1 ha and lake pumps need an RWB permit [V LOC-04]. Whether a small drip block under 1 ha needs a permit is [U] |
| Establishment | Young trees are watered on a written rota in the first dry season (June–August 2027), litres recorded per block (`WaterEnergy`) |
| Method | Hand or bucket at first; basin around the tree under the mulch; drip once installed (permit decision first, T36). Drip is expensive for 1–10 ha farms; subsidies of 50–75% exist if eligible [V CRP-35, U eligibility] |
| Time | Early morning or evening; never on a hot midday |
| Check | A finger test of soil moisture 10–15 cm deep before and after; soil-moisture sensors (T52) supplement and never replace the hand test during the pilot |
| Do not | Waterlog avocado; let basins fill with stagnant water |

## 5. Mulching, weeds and soil

- Mulch a ring around each tree out to the drip line, kept a few centimetres off the trunk; renew it at the start of each rainy season and before the long dry [A].
- Weeds are controlled by hand, slashing and mulch. Chemicals only on the agronomist's written advice.
- Compost and composted manure only; raw manure never goes on fruit trees close to harvest (zoning concept rule 4).
- Soil re-test per block once a year in the dry season [A], compared with the lab baseline from T06.
- Contours and cover: gullies and bare patches get grass strips, mulch or check dams to hold soil and keep it out of the lake.

## 6. Pruning and training (by crop)

| Crop | Practice |
|---|---|
| Avocado | Formative pruning in years 1–3 to shape a strong frame; remove dead and crossing wood; keep the canopy open for air and light (a crowded 5 x 5 m layout needs earlier pruning [E CRP]); sanitise tools between trees; times set by the agronomist |
| Mango | Light formative pruning; open the canopy after harvest; keep the tree at a height that can be picked; flowering needs a dry spell of at least three months [V CRP-34], so do not irrigate heavily in the flowering window |
| Citrus (trial block) | Remove suckers below the graft; light shaping; tools cleaned between trees |
| Passion fruit | Train on the trellis; prune and thin as advised; remove and burn vines that show woodiness symptoms; replant every 2–4 years [A]; cutting tools cleaned between vines [A, confirm with the agronomist] |
| Papaya | Thin to the vigorous plants; remove diseased plants early; short-lived, replant on a plan [A] |
| Tree tomato | Highland crop only [E CRP]; the farm lists it only if the site is cool enough (15–20 C [V CRP-34]) |
| Macadamia | Option only; low prices and one named buyer [V CRP] |
| Fodder trees (Calliandra, Gliricidia, Leucaena) | Pollard or coppice at the heights the agronomist sets; used as a supplement, never the main feed (Calliandra 20–40% of the diet [V LVS-21]) |
| Boundary timber (Grevillea, Markhamia) | Planted from RFA seedlings; prune lower branches; no felling without the permit route [V CRP-03, CRP-04, CRP-06] |

Dates are set by the agronomist for the site; the farm keeps a pruning log per block.

## 7. Integrated pest and disease management (IPM) scouting

**Weekly scouting walk** (Tuesday in the SOP): a fixed route, 10 trees per block, the same trees each time.

| Look for | Applies to | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Yellowing, wilting, dieback, collar lesions, gumming | Avocado (root rot [V CRP-44]) | Improve drainage; mark the tree; report to the agronomist; no replanting in the same spot without advice |
| Fruit flies: stung fruit, larvae, falling fruit | Mango, avocado, citrus; "traps hard to access" in Rwandan mango farms [V CRP-09, CRP-44] | Sanitation (remove and bury fallen fruit), monitoring traps, harvest on time; control methods per the agronomist; some export markets refuse fruit with flies [V CRP-44] |
| Virus symptoms: mosaic, woodiness, distorted fruit | Passion fruit [V CRP-33] | Remove the plant, keep stock certified, clean tools; report counts |
| Banana Xanthomonas wilt | Banana (where kept for food or livestock use) | Remove diseased plants; clean tools; avoid sharing planting material [V CRP-53] |
| Termites, ants, leaf-cutting insects, scale | All young trees | Mulch management; the agronomist's advice |
| Mammal and bird damage | Young trees, seedlings | Guards; fence; watch routine |
| Cattle, goat or sheep browsing | Young trees | Fence line repair; keep animals out |
| Napier stunt or Brachiaria problems | Fodder (stunt disease data for Rwanda [U LVS]) | Report symptoms; replace with tolerant cultivars as advised |

Each scouting entry is a count, not an opinion: date, block, trees checked, number affected, symptom, photo, action. The farm sets treatment thresholds with the agronomist [U] and records every treatment (product, rate, date, pre-harvest interval, operator, protective equipment).

## 8. Fertiliser and soil amendments

- Applications follow the lab results and the agronomist's written rates; quantities in the cost studies (0.25 kg per hole, 0.5 kg per tree per year in years 1–3 [A CRP]) are placeholders only.
- Fertiliser subsidies cover fruit trees [V CRP-46]; eligibility of this farm is [U] (T27).
- Compost: kept covered, turned, labelled by date; used as the main source of organic matter.
- Every application is in `CropOps`: block, product, rate, date, who.

## 9. Harvest and post-harvest, by crop type

| Crop | Harvest | Post-harvest | Buyer link |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Avocado (Hass, Fuerte)** | Mature fruit picked with the pedicel button; timing by the exporter's spec (dry matter: the Australian minimum for Hass is 23%; Rwandan threshold [U]) [V CRP-44, CRP-45]; counts by size; harvest in the cool part of the day | Shade immediately; no fruit on the ground; sort by grade; for Kigali a short shaded chain is enough [E], for export a cold chain through an exporter [V CRP-19, CRP-20]; ripens in 4–14 days [V CRP-44] | Exporter or aggregator under contract; Afri-Farmers Market-type buyers who collect [V BUY-03]; supply season about September–March [V CRP-19] |
| **Mango** | Picked by hand at the stage the buyer needs; no shaking; fruit-fly sanitation first | Hard to store, so sell within days; farmers sell by the sack and often at "giveaway prices" in gluts [V CRP-09]; the plan has a buyer before the harvest | Traders, Kigali markets, a processor if one is found [U] |
| **Passion fruit** | Picked ripe or just-fallen as the buyer specifies; frequent small picks | Clean, sort, cool and shade; fresh export favours Rwandan purple passion fruit [V CRP-30]; volumes small | Exporters (NAEB lists 17 for passion fruit [V BUY-31]) or Kigali buyers |
| **Papaya** | Harvest by colour stage; handle gently | Short shelf life; no processing outlet verified [U] | Local market; only with a buyer agreed |
| **Citrus** | Trial block first; picked when ripe | Cooler storage helps; import substitution is the case for Kigali supply [V CRP-18, CRP-10] | Supermarkets, traders |
| **Tree tomato, macadamia** | Only if selected | As the agronomist advises | — |
| **Fodder grass** | Cut at about 90 days for the best protein and energy balance [V LVS-15, LVS-17]; Napier every 6–8 weeks [V LVS-52]; Chloris for hay at or before flowering [V LVS-18] | Hay: dry, baled or stacked off the ground and under cover; silage only with a proper seal; weigh and record | Internal; surplus hay can be sold (RAB sells hay to private farms [V LVS-50]) |
| **Boundary timber** | Not harvested before the permit route is known [V CRP-03, CRP-04, CRP-06] | — | — |

Common rules: clean crates, harvest in the cool part of the day, shade within an hour, sort to the buyer's spec, label with block and date, weigh, record. A harvest with no record is not sold.

## 10. Food safety and traceability

- Hands washed before harvest; the toilet and hand-wash point are away from the packing table; animals kept away from the packing area.
- Each box is traceable to block and date (`Harvest`), so a buyer's complaint can be traced to a block and a spray record.
- Buyers may require GlobalG.A.P.; exporters use it [V CRP-20]. A small farm normally sells through an exporter or aggregator [V CRP-20]; the gap assessment is T74.

## 11. Waste and the lake

- Pruning waste is chipped for mulch or composted; diseased material is burned or buried away from water.
- Empty chemical containers (if any are used) are returned to the supplier or disposed of as the agronomist advises, never rinsed into the lake or a stream.
- Silt traps and grass strips on the lake side of the orchard.

## 12. Fodder, pasture and test plots

- Test two or three cultivars in small plots for a year before committing hectares [E from LVS-15, LVS-16]; record cut weights (kg fresh from a 1 m2 quadrat is a start; the Bugesera trials used quadrat samples [V LVS-15, LVS-16]) and survival.
- Candidate base species for the East: Brachiaria hybrids, Napier, Chloris gayana; legumes such as lablab; fodder trees as supplement. See the species table in `../research/livestock-market-research.md` section 9.2.
- Establishment: first cut about 90 days after planting; light grazing at 3–4 months for Brachiaria [V LVS-25].
- Dry-season reserve: hay and silage, with a measured weight in store; the model conserves 30% of the surplus for M1 [A]. A 100-goat herd at maintenance needs roughly 150–330 L of water a day [E LVS-27].
- Illustration of stocking: 7–15 local adult goats per hectare of usable forage under the stated assumptions [E LVS 9.4]; the farm measures its own forage and revises.
