# Decision log

Every material decision about how the study is run, or what the farm will do, gets an entry. When a recommendation changes, a new entry says why, and the old one is not edited. Newest entries go at the bottom.

Format: **ID · date · decision** — context · alternatives considered · consequence · revisit when.

---

**D-001 · 2026-10-19 · The study lives in this repo under `study/`, next to the future website in `src/`.**
Context: the folder was a fresh Next.js 16 app created for the farm. Alternatives: a separate repo for the study; top-level folders as in the brief's sample layout. Chosen because one repo keeps the study and the site that will present it together without mixing the two (internal working documents vs public content). Consequence: the repo must stay private while it holds supplier, land and financial details. Revisit: if the website repo ever needs to become public, split `study/` out first.

**D-002 · 2026-10-19 · Every claim carries an evidence label: [V], [E], [A] or [U].**
Context: the brief forbids invented facts and asks to separate verified facts from estimates and assumptions. The labels, the source-ID registers and the no-outreach rule are in `CLAUDE.md`, so every later session and research agent works to the same standard. Consequence: unlabelled numbers are defects, and the research files are checked for them before synthesis.

**D-003 · 2026-10-19 · Run the work in stages; Stage 1 covers discovery and desk research only.**
Context: the brief defines seven stages and lists the Stage 1 deliverables. The farm's location is unconfirmed, so site-specific design would be fiction. Consequence: nothing site-specific is claimed; findings are framed by candidate zone; the website waits for Stage 6. Revisit: when the founder sends the pin or UPI.

**D-004 · 2026-10-19 · The founder's planning answers become constraints, not preferences.**
Context: asked during planning, the founder answered: location "not sure yet", area 2–10 ha, capital under USD 25k for years 1–2, land owned by him or his company. The brief says not to plan for the lowest possible budget, but a stated capital limit is a fact to design around. Consequence: the decision matrix weights initial investment (8) and time to first revenue (7); the financial model computes a funding gap for each farm model; ambitious lines are costed with a financing path rather than dropped.

**D-005 · 2026-10-19 · Desk research runs as five parallel research agents; synthesis and verification stay in the main session.**
Context: location, markets, crops, livestock and buyers are independent topics. Agents ran on the research model tier set in the founder's engine config, with an identical evidence brief; each wrote only its own files. Consequence: the main session re-checks the decision-critical claims (prices used in the model, yields, legal buffers, supplier existence) before they enter the synthesis.

**D-006 · 2026-10-19 · The financial model is generated by a script, with a monthly engine.**
Context: the brief asks for a 24-month monthly cash flow and annual projections of at least five years. Tree crops pay late (avocado and macadamia beyond year 5), so the model runs 120 months and summarises 10 farm years. It is in real 2026 RWF with no inflation, computes cash before financing, and has no terminal value (conservative). The sale age, first-birth lag and receivable lag are fixed in the formula structure and changed only by rebuilding. Alternatives: a hand-built spreadsheet, harder to keep correct and to regenerate. Consequence: `scripts/build_financial_model.py` is the source of truth for structure; inputs can be edited in the workbook or in the script, with changes recorded here.

**D-007 · 2026-10-19 · Stage 1 does not recommend a farm model.**
Context: site suitability carries 24% of the decision-matrix weight and is unknown. Consequence: the three models are defined and costed with placeholders; the recommendation is a Stage 3 deliverable, gated on site data, buyer interviews and real costs.

**D-008 · 2026-10-19 · Model inputs updated from research; the avocado curve is cut by about 75%.**
Context: the placeholder avocado curve reached 110 kg per tree by year 10. The crop research found no Rwandan yield-by-age data and proposes half an Australian irrigated benchmark (about 24 kg per tree at maturity, CRP-45), with irrigated Rwandan exporters at 12–15 t/ha (CRP-20). The base curve now follows the planning case, with an upside multiplier of 2.0. Prices now come from e-Soko medians (MKT-03), and FX from the BNR (MKT-04). Consequence: every model shows a funding gap in the base case, and the binding question becomes overheads vs scale, not technology. Revisit: when Rwandan orchard records or quotes arrive.

**D-009 · 2026-10-19 · Livestock house placement follows the 200 m kraal rule; livestock purchase depends on RVF status.**
Context: Law 48/2018 Art 42(6), re-read in the gazette text, bars kraals within 200 m of lake banks. An RVF quarantine has covered the Eastern Province since 2026-06-26. Consequence: the zoning must place Z3 housing at least 200 m from the lake; the livestock gate (G4) requires the quarantine to be lifted in the farm's district.

**D-010 · 2026-10-19 · Model inputs follow the Stage 4 farm design (zone A base).**
Context: the design found that feed caps the herd at about 20 does on 5 ha (not 40–60) and that water caps the orchard. It also found no sheep or passion fruit in the eastern zones (low demand; woodiness virus) and that pole thinning needs a special permit. Consequence: the M1 gap falls to RWF 4.6M and EBITDA turns positive in year 7; M2 turns positive in year 2. No model closes the gap in the base case. Revisit: when the location is confirmed (zone D changes crops and sheep) and when quotes arrive.

**D-011 · 2026-10-19 · No external capital now; self-funded validation first.**
Context: the financing strategy (`../investment/financing-strategy.md`) and the sensitivity runs agree. Capital costs and prices dominate, and there is no evidence yet (site, title, buyers). Consequence: the pitch deck asks for nothing. Spending is released in tranches as gates pass; outside money is reconsidered once there is a validated pilot.

**D-012 · 2026-10-19 · `/docs` must stay private.**
Context: the docs site renders buyer contacts, supplier leads, draft outreach and the finances. Consequence: never deploy `/docs` publicly. The Stage 6 public website is a separate route set; gate or exclude `/docs` before any deploy.

**D-013 · 2026-10-19 · The study is public; supersedes D-012.**
Context: the founder decided to publish the study openly in the databayt org (public repo `databayt/farm`) and to serve `/docs` publicly at https://farm.databayt.org. It includes buyer and supplier contacts as published by those businesses, draft outreach and the finances. Consequence: the docs are deployed as a static site on Cloudflare (Workers Static Assets, Worker `farm`); nothing private (no `.env`, no credentials) is in the repo. Revisit: before adding anything that is not meant to be public, such as negotiated prices, a counterpart's private details or the founder's personal documents.

**D-014 · 2026-10-26 · Fit out the three existing 20 ft containers as identical farm-stay guest studios with an IKEA-style flat-pack interior, costed outside the farm model.**
Context: three standard 20 ft containers already stand on the site on raised steel decks with identical openings (a 3 m sliding glass wall and a narrow steel door). The founder asked for compact studios (bed, shower, WC, kitchenette), materials bought and made in Kigali, a flat-pack build in the IKEA manner, and a roof terrace under a transparent shade; the founder reports the units are already approved. Alternatives: staff housing, founder-only use, or a turnkey conversion (listed at RWF 24-42M per house, STU-P04). Consequence: a new study section (`study/studio/`) with a parametric generator (`scripts/studio/`) that draws the plans, the 3D model, the cut lists and the bill of materials from one source. The studios' cost and payback are shown on their own and stay out of `financial-model.xlsx` (D-011) until the founder commits funds. Revisit: after the site survey and the first three quotes.

**D-015 · 2026-10-26 · Layout A: bathroom at the narrow-door end, bed across the far end; the WC faces across the width; the water heater is 80 L, not 50 L.**
Context: the founder chose Layout A from three options. A geometry check showed that a WC facing along the container fails if the glazing ends more than about 70 mm later than the photo suggests, so the fixtures are arranged to tolerate 150 mm: the WC sits on the old-door infill facing across the width, a 1,200 x 900 walk-in shower runs along the back wall, the basin is on the end wall, and the wardrobe absorbs the slack. The heater was first proposed at 50 L; two back-to-back showers need about 48-64 L of hot water against about 35 L usable from 50 L, so the specification is 80 L with a 6-7 L/min shower head and a thermostatic mixer. Alternatives: Layout B (the mirror image) and Layout C (keep the narrow door as an entrance; it failed the clearances). Consequence: the narrow door becomes a frosted louvre window and all plumbing sits in one wet core at end B. Revisit: if the survey finds the openings more than 150 mm from the photo estimate.

**D-016 · 2026-10-26 · No guests until the septic system passes inspection; WASAC water reaches the storage tank through an air gap.**
Context: there is no septic system on site. The rules found put septic tanks and soak pits at least 50 m from a borehole (2012 regulations, STU-11) and ask for a percolation test (DRS 143 draft, STU-10). The site also has a borehole and rainwater, which must never back-feed the WASAC main. Consequence: the septic tank, soak pits and grease trap are built and tested before the first booking; WASAC fills the tank through an air gap above the overflow, and the private sources feed the same tank, never the main. Revisit: when the site plan and the percolation test are available.
