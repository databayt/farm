## What the model answers

1. How much capital each farm model needs, and when it is spent.
2. Its **peak funding need**: the lowest point of cumulative cash, which includes the operating losses before the trees bear.
3. Its **funding gap** against the founder's capital (under USD 25k, about RWF 35M in the base case).
4. When EBITDA turns positive, when cumulative cash pays back, and the 10-year NPV and IRR.
5. Which assumptions move those answers most (Stage 3 sensitivity runs).

It does not answer whether the farm *should* do something; the decision matrix and field evidence do that.

## How it is built

| Sheet | Role |
|---|---|
| Cover | Status, legend, structural constants, what is not modelled |
| Control | Scenario switch (downside / base / upside), farm-model switch (M1 / M2 / M3), area, capital, start and planting months |
| Assumptions | Every uncertain input with downside / base / upside values, label and source |
| Models | What defines M1, M2 and M3: land shares, orchard mix, herd plan, capital items, workers |
| Curves | Yield per plant by year, harvest seasonality by month, pasture growth by month |
| Sensitivity | Multipliers for prices, yields, mortality, feed, labour, capex, transport, plus a tree-maturity delay |
| Land | Areas by zone and crop; boundary length |
| Trees | Annual orchard engine: plants, stand, yield, production, sales, replanting; boundary poles |
| Livestock | Monthly herd engine per species: purchases, births, deaths, culls, replacements, sales, value, feed demand |
| Feed | Monthly dry-matter balance: forage grown, surplus conserved as hay/silage, deficit bought |
| CapEx | Capital items with essential/optional flag, month incurred, life and depreciation; contingency |
| CashFlow | 120 monthly columns (months 1–24 are the monthly view the brief asks for): sales, collections, operating costs, investment, tax, net and cumulative cash, balance against the founder's capital |
| Annual | 10 farm years: revenue by line, direct costs, gross margin, overheads, EBITDA, depreciation, tax, net profit, cash flow, discount factors, payback and break-even markers, operating indicators |
| Metrics | Headline results and the tie-out checks |

Conventions:
- Constant 2026 RWF (real terms) with a real discount rate.
- Month 1 is the first month of spending (November 2026). A farm year is 12 model months (November–October).
- Cash flows are before financing.
- Blue cells are inputs, black cells formulas, green cells links. Every input row carries its evidence label and source.

**Structural constants** (fixed in the formula shape; change them in the script and rebuild): young stock sold at 9 months; first birth 6 months after a female joins; credit sales collected one month later.

## Simplifications to keep in mind

- No loans or interest, no inflation, no loss carry-forward for tax, no terminal value for orchards. The NPV is therefore conservative for long-lived trees. The standing value of boundary timber at year 10 is shown as a memo only.
- Livestock: herd numbers are expected values (fractions of animals). Males are added as the herd grows and never die. Births start 6 months after the herd count they come from.
- Fruit seasonality is uniform until a Rwandan harvest calendar per crop replaces it. This affects the monthly cash pattern, not annual totals.
- Hay/silage storage has no capacity limit or spoilage beyond the conservation efficiency.
- Interplanting (e.g. passion fruit between young avocados) is not modelled: crops occupy separate blocks.

**Reading:**
- On 5 ha, about RWF 3M a year of fixed overheads absorbs the revenue of a 1.25 ha avocado block plus a 40-doe herd. The result turns on:
  - yields (irrigated vs rain-fed avocado);
  - selling above farm-gate;
  - cheaper overheads;
  - farm size.
- M2's early positive EBITDA comes from passion fruit and papaya. It erodes as replanting and bought feed rise (feed self-sufficiency 49% in year 5).
- M3's technology adds cost without, so far, any modelled yield or price benefit. That benefit has to be evidenced before it is modelled.

**Reading [E]:**
- Capital cost is the single largest lever, so real quotes and phasing matter more than any agronomic assumption.
- Livestock and fruit prices come next. Selling above farm-gate is the cheapest improvement available.
- No single ±20% change makes M1 viable. Farm size alone doesn't either, because costs scale with area.
- The diversified model on more land has the best NPV, at the price of a larger funding need. That points to a validated pilot first, then scaling with outside money once there is evidence.
