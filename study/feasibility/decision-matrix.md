# Decision matrix — framework

Status: Stage 1 — criteria, weights and models defined; scores provisional · Created: 2026-10-19
Location dependency: the site-fit criteria (24% of the weight) cannot be scored until the location and soil/water results exist.

This matrix compares business lines, and through them farm models, on transparent, weighted criteria. Weights are judgments, so each one carries its reason. Changing a weight is allowed but gets an entry in `../execution/decision-log.md`.

## How scoring works

1. **Gates first.** A line that fails any gate is excluded, whatever its score:
   - G1 **Legal at the site**: nothing in the land title, land-use plan, lake or wetland buffer or permit rules forbids it.
   - G2 **Site suitability ≥ 3**: the climate, altitude and soil fit (needs the location).
   - G3 **A buyer channel with evidence**: at least one named channel buys this product in Kigali or nearby, shown by research or interviews.
   - G4 **Fundable**: its establishment fits the phased capital, or a credible financing path is identified.
2. **Score each criterion 1–5** using the anchors below. Each score records its evidence label: `[V]` with a source ID, `[E]` with the method, `[A]` provisional.
3. **Weighted score** = Σ (weight × score) / 5, out of 100.
4. **Rank lines, then assemble models.** The farm models below are combinations of lines and are judged on their combined score plus the financial model's outputs: peak funding need, funding gap, payback and NPV.

## Criteria and weights

| # | Criterion | Weight | Why this weight |
|---|---|---:|---|
| 1 | Suitability for the actual location | 10 | The binding constraint; a crop that doesn't fit the site fails regardless of market. Also a gate. |
| 2 | Strength of demonstrated local demand | 10 | The brief requires the farm to be designed around real buyers. |
| 3 | Access to reliable buyers | 9 | Demand that the farm cannot reach (volume, quality, payment terms) is not revenue. |
| 4 | Expected profitability (margin per ha and per RWF invested) | 9 | The farm must pay back; judged from the financial model, not headline prices. |
| 5 | Initial investment | 8 | Capital is under USD 25k for two years (founder, 2026-10-19), so expensive establishment must earn its place. |
| 6 | Time to first revenue | 7 | Same capital limit: early cash flow reduces the funding gap and validates buyers sooner. |
| 7 | Water requirements | 6 | Water is the likeliest physical limit, and lake water may need a permit or be off-limits. |
| 8 | Climate resilience | 5 | Rainfall is erratic in parts of eastern Rwanda (to be confirmed per zone); drought tolerance protects the investment. |
| 9 | Pest and disease risk | 5 | Several candidate crops and both livestock species have serious disease pressures in the region. |
| 10 | Annual operating costs | 4 | Recurrent cost erodes margin; partly captured in profitability, hence a lower weight. |
| 11 | Labour requirements | 4 | Daily-care lines need reliable staff and supervision; the founder is not on site every day. |
| 12 | Time to full production | 3 | Matters for long-cycle crops, but the time to first revenue already carries most of the cash-flow concern. |
| 13 | Soil requirements | 3 | Many soil limits can be corrected (lime, organic matter) at a cost; the gate and suitability carry the rest. |
| 14 | Post-harvest complexity | 3 | Perishable products need fast transport or cold chain; a risk to quality and price. |
| 15 | Price volatility | 3 | Seasonal gluts hurt; partly managed through variety choice and buyer contracts. |
| 16 | Scalability | 3 | Whether the line can grow on more land or with processing later. |
| 17 | Compatibility with livestock | 3 | Integration (manure, shade, browse) is a core idea of the farm, but must not damage trees. |
| 18 | Environmental impact | 3 | Lake proximity, erosion and biodiversity; also a reputational asset for the brand. |
| 19 | Fit with the founder's engineering skills | 2 | A tie-breaker: technology should serve the farm, not pick its crops. |
| | **Total** | **100** | |

Weight by theme: market 19 · capital and timing 18 · site fit 24 (location 10 + water 6 + climate 5 + soil 3) · economics 13 (profitability 9 + opex 4) · operations and risk 15 (pests 5 + labour 4 + post-harvest 3 + volatility 3) · strategic 11 (scalability 3 + livestock 3 + environment 3 + founder fit 2).

## Scoring anchors (5 = best)

Numeric anchors that depend on costs (initial investment, opex, profitability) are set in Stage 3 from the financial model, relative to the range across candidate lines. The structural anchors are:

| Criterion | 5 | 3 | 1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Time to first revenue | < 12 months | 24–36 months | > 60 months |
| Time to full production | < 2 years | 4–6 years | > 8 years |
| Water requirements | Rain-fed works in the zone | Supplementary irrigation in dry seasons | Full irrigation year-round |
| Labour | Seasonal, low | Weekly tasks plus peaks | Daily, skilled |
| Post-harvest | Stores weeks at ambient | Days; careful handling | Hours; needs cold chain |
| Demand | Documented, large and growing; several buyer types | Documented but niche or seasonal | Anecdotal only |
| Buyers | Several named buyers buy direct from farms | Mostly through traders | No identified route |
| Pests/diseases | Few, manageable with standard practice | Known risks with established controls | Severe and poorly controlled locally |
| Climate resilience | Tolerates drought and heavy rain | Sensitive in one season | Fails in a normal dry season |
| Livestock compatibility | Benefits from or with animals | Neutral if separated | Damaged by or damages animals |

## Farm models

Each model is a combination of lines. The financial model (`financial-model.xlsx`, switch on the `Control` sheet) computes each model's capital need, peak funding need and funding gap against the under-USD-25k envelope.

| | M1 Conservative mixed | M2 Diversified commercial | M3 Tech-enabled integrated |
|---|---|---|---|
| Idea | Prove one fruit line and a goat herd cheaply | Add fast-cash fruit and a second species; irrigate the orchard | The full integrated farm with automation and value-adding |
| Fruit | One proven species (best evidence for the site) | Two or three species, including a fast-cash line | Several species plus seedlings for sale |
| Livestock | Goats (meat and young stock) | Goats plus sheep if demand supports it | Goats and sheep with breeding-stock sales |
| Feed | Paddocks plus a fodder bank; buy feed in the dry season if needed | Plus hay/silage conservation | Plus conservation and measured grazing |
| Water | Rain harvesting plus storage; hand-watering during establishment | Plus a solar pump and drip on the orchard | Plus a borehole if legal, plus automated drip |
| Technology | Phone-based records, a livestock scale, a few DIY sensors (tank level, rain) | A low-cost sensor network (soil moisture, tank level, flow) built by the founder | A LoRa network, an automated irrigation controller, the farm platform with an AI assistant, cameras |
| Capital profile | Designed to fit inside the envelope, phased | Likely at or above the envelope; phased over two years | Beyond the envelope; needs outside finance once M1/M2 lines are validated |
| What must be true | One fruit and goats suit the site; buyers exist | Water is available and legal for drip; fast-cash fruit sells | Lines are validated; financing secured; operating processes stable |

The recommendation is made in Stage 3, once site data, buyer interviews and the costed models exist. Stage 1 does not pick a model.
