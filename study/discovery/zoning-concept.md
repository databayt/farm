# Preliminary zoning concept

Status: Stage 1 — principles and provisional shares only; **no site data yet** · Created: 2026-10-19
Location dependency: site-specific. Every share and placement below is `[A]` until the location, survey and site visit exist. No parcel boundaries are drawn or implied.

## Placement principles

The layout follows how often each area needs attention and where water and protection are:

| Zone | What goes there | Placement rule | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Z0 Base | House, store, workshop, feed store, compost | Near the access road and the best water point; existing buildings first | Daily traffic, security, deliveries |
| Z1 Intensive | Small nursery, seedling hardening area, demonstration and trial beds | Next to Z0 and the water | Needs daily watering and watching |
| Z2 Orchard blocks | Fruit trees, by species or block | Best soils, gentle slopes, good drainage; kept out of frost pockets and waterlogged spots; uphill of livestock runoff | Highest value per hectare; longest commitment |
| Z3 Livestock | Goat/sheep house, paddocks for rotational grazing, a fodder bank (cut-and-carry grass on contours), a quarantine pen | Dry, raised ground for housing; paddocks on medium land; quarantine pen away from the main herd and the neighbours' animals | Health, manure collection, controlled grazing |
| Z4 Boundary and shelter | Boundary timber or pole trees, live fence, windbreaks, fodder trees | Perimeter and contour lines | Security, wind, future timber value, browse |
| Z5 Buffer and conservation | The legal lake or wetland buffer, steep slopes, gullies, wet spots | Wherever the law and the land dictate | Legal compliance, erosion control, biodiversity, pollinators |

## Rules where trees and livestock meet [A], to confirm with research and a vet or agronomist

1. **No grazing inside young orchard blocks.** Fruit trees are fenced off from goats and sheep until they are established. The safe tree age and height per species is set in the Stage 4 design.
2. **Goats never roam free near trees.** They browse bark and seedlings. Grazing is in fenced paddocks only, with fodder brought to the house.
3. **Sheep may graze under mature orchard rows** only where research supports it for that species, and only with guards on the trunks.
4. **Manure from the animal house is composted before it goes on fruit trees.** It never goes raw near fruit close to harvest, for food safety.
5. **Shade for animals** comes from boundary and fodder trees, not from the productive orchard.
6. **No livestock access to the lake or wetland buffer** unless research confirms it is lawful, and even then the water is piped to troughs to protect banks and animal health.

## Decisions that cannot responsibly be made without site data

| Decision | Blocked on |
|---|---|
| Which fruit species and cultivars | Altitude, rainfall, temperature, soil test results |
| Where orchard blocks go | Slope, drainage, soil depth by zone |
| Irrigation design and its cost | Water source, its flow and quality, legal right to abstract, elevation differences |
| Whether a borehole is worth drilling | Hydrogeology, the permit process, neighbours' experience |
| Pasture species and stocking rate | Rainfall pattern, soils, measured forage growth |
| Herd size | Paddock and fodder area, dry-season feed plan, water for animals |
| Fence lengths and cost | Surveyed boundary and paddock layout |
| Building sites and sizes | Existing buildings, flood risk, access |
| Usable area net of the buffer | Shoreline position, the legal buffer width, survey |
| Power design (grid vs solar) | REG grid distance, actual loads |
| Sensor and network design | Terrain, signal survey, farm size, chosen systems |
