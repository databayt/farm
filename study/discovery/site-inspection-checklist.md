# Site inspection checklist

Status: Stage 1 — ready to use · Created: 2026-10-19
Purpose: one structured visit (a full day; two people is better) that turns the farm from an unknown into a measured baseline. Nothing in this study is site-specific until this visit and the farm questionnaire are done.

## Before the visit

- [ ] Questionnaire P1 answers in hand (pin, title, area, water).
- [ ] Water-sampling bottles collected from the chosen lab. Bacteriology bottles must be sterile and analysed within the lab's time limit; ask the lab for its instructions.
- [ ] Soil sample bags and labels (see `soil-water-testing-plan.md`).
- [ ] Weather: go in daylight with no heavy rain forecast. A dry-season visit shows the worst-case water situation.
- [ ] Tell a neighbour or local leader you are coming. They are also the best informants on water and past land use.

## 1. Boundary and area

- [ ] Walk the boundary with GPS tracking on. Mark every corner or beacon as a waypoint.
- [ ] Compare the measured area with the title area and note the difference.
- [ ] Mark the gate, internal paths and any communal paths crossing the land.
- [ ] Record neighbours' uses on each side (crops, grazing, houses, wetland, lake).

## 2. Lake and buffer

- [ ] Find the shoreline and the visible high-water mark nearest the farm. Measure the horizontal distance to the nearest boundary point and to any planned building or planting.
- [ ] Note wetland vegetation (papyrus, reeds) and flood marks; they hint at the legal buffer and flood risk.
- [ ] Note who uses the shore: fishing, washing, livestock watering, public access points.

## 3. Topography and erosion

- [ ] Split the farm into visibly different zones (upper slope, mid slope, valley bottom, flat). Record the slope in % for each.
- [ ] Map gullies, rills, bare patches, terraces and flood or waterlogged spots.
- [ ] Note the highest point near the farm, a possible gravity-fed tank site.

## 4. Soils (with the testing plan)

- [ ] For each zone, auger or dig to 1 m (or to rock or hardpan). Record depth, texture by feel (sandy / loamy / clayey), colour, mottling (grey or orange spots mean poor drainage), stones, roots and termite activity.
- [ ] Take the composite samples described in `soil-water-testing-plan.md`.
- [ ] Run three single-ring infiltration tests (good soil, poor soil, a typical spot). Record the water drop every 5 min for 30–60 min.

## 5. Water

For each source (borehole, well, spring, stream, lake, tank, piped supply):
- [ ] GPS point, type, owner or user, distance to the farm centre.
- [ ] Flow test (litres per second, 3 repeats) or depth to water.
- [ ] Look, smell, and turbidity; nearby contamination (latrines, cattle, fuel, rubbish).
- [ ] Take lab samples where the plan says so.
- [ ] Ask neighbours: does it dry up, and when? Has it ever been contaminated?
- [ ] Measure the roof areas of existing buildings for rainwater harvesting.

## 6. Vegetation and existing trees

- [ ] Existing trees: species, count, approximate age, health; anything worth keeping (shade, windbreak, fruit).
- [ ] Ground cover: grass species if known, weeds, invasive plants; signs of overgrazing.
- [ ] Pests seen: termites, fruit flies, signs of crop disease on neighbouring farms.

## 7. Infrastructure and services

- [ ] Buildings: use, dimensions, wall and roof condition, doors and locks.
- [ ] Fencing: type, length, condition; gates.
- [ ] Electricity: grid on site? The nearest pole or transformer (GPS point); meter type.
- [ ] Mobile signal: bars and a speed test at the gate, the centre and the far corner, for MTN and Airtel.
- [ ] Road: distance to tarmac, surface, slope; whether a loaded pickup could pass in the rainy season.

## 8. Livestock suitability

- [ ] Natural shade and shelter sites; a dry, raised spot for a goat house.
- [ ] Neighbours' livestock: species, numbers, health; shared grazing or water (a disease-exposure risk).
- [ ] Predators (dogs and others) and theft risk; a place for a night enclosure in sight of the house.

## 9. People and community

- [ ] Local labour available; typical daily wage.
- [ ] Local leaders and the sector agronomist and veterinarian (names and roles only; no commitments made).
- [ ] Any tension, disputes or expectations from the community about the land.

## 10. Standard photo set (all GPS-tagged)

- [ ] From each corner, looking inward and along both boundaries.
- [ ] From the centre: north, east, south, west.
- [ ] Each water source, each building, the access road, the lakeshore.
- [ ] Each soil pit (with a tape for scale).

## Outputs of the visit

1. GPX/KML boundary track and waypoints.
2. This checklist, filled in.
3. A photo log (file name → what it shows).
4. A soil and water sample list with chain-of-custody details (see the testing plan).
5. A one-page "surprises" note: anything that contradicts the questionnaire or this study's assumptions.

These feed `zoning-concept.md`, the Stage 4 farm design and the financial model's area, water and capex inputs.
