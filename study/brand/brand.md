# Brand — positioning, voice and identity direction

Status: Stage 6 draft · Date: 2026-10-19 · The farm has **no name yet** and its **location is unconfirmed**.
This is a direction document. Nothing here is a claim to be published. Every public statement must pass the claims ladder in §9.

## 1. What the brand can honestly stand on today

| Brand pillar (from the brief) | What exists now | What it can say now | What it must wait for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Product quality | Nothing grown | Nothing | Graded lots delivered and accepted (gate C3) |
| Natural and responsible farming | A design intent: trees with livestock, soil cover, water care [A] | "We are designing the farm around soil and water care" (a plan, labelled as a plan) | Practices in place and recorded; any term like "natural" defined in writing |
| Reliability | Nothing delivered | Nothing | Repeat deliveries on time (gate C4) |
| Agricultural innovation, engineering, technology | A study, a model and a record system plan | "We measure and record, and publish our progress" | Sensors or automation only if justified by evidence; the model shows technology (M3) widens the gap without proven benefit |
| Traceability | A lot-code design (`../go-to-market/sales-plan.md`) | "Every lot will carry a code" once the first lot does | Real lot records |
| Long-term sustainability | A 5 ha farm design with a lake buffer to respect (50 m, 200 m) [V LOC-02] | The farm will keep the legal lake buffers [A, once site mapped] | Measured soil, water and cost results |

The honest brand story today is **a farm being built in the open, with its numbers shown**: what is known, what is estimated, what is still unknown. That is a real point of difference (competitors publish no farm-gate prices or outgrower terms [V buyer database §3]) and it needs no invented claim.

## 2. Name options (the farm has none)

Rules: not a place name (location unconfirmed; "Kuroshira" matches nothing in Rwanda's village list [E LOC]); short; spoken easily in Kinyarwanda, English and French; no claim in the name ("Organic", "Eco", "Premium"); no existing-brand look-alikes. **Availability is not checked [U]**: RDB name search, trademark search and domain checks are required before adoption.

Kinyarwanda meanings below are the founder's to confirm with a native speaker [A]; they are common words, not a translation service.

| # | Option | Idea | Pros | Cons / checks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Imbuto** | "seed, fruit" | Short; fits fruit and growth; easy to say | Very common word; likely taken as a brand [U] |
| 2 | **Umusaruro** | "harvest, produce" | Describes what a buyer wants | Long for logos; sounds generic |
| 3 | **Ikiyaga Farm** | "lake" + farm | Evokes the setting | Implies location before it is confirmed; the lake is a legal constraint, not the product |
| 4 | **Isoko Farm** | "market, source, spring" | Double meaning: source of produce and a market; e-Soko users know the word | Similar to e-Soko (MINAGRI system); confusion risk [U] |
| 5 | **Umurima** | "field, farm" | Simple, plain | Generic |
| 6 | **Tree & Flock** (English working name) | The integrated model of trees and livestock | Clear; works in English-led B2B | Not Rwandan in feel; "Flock" suits sheep more than goats |
| 7 | **Open Orchard** (English working name) | Orchard that shows its numbers | Matches the transparency story | Orchard-only; excludes goats |

Recommendation: **do not fix a name until the location, title and first crops are known** (G0 and G2). Use the working label `[FARM NAME]` in all documents. When choosing: shortlist three; check RDB company and trademark registers, domain and social handles; test with three buyers in interviews (does it sound like a real supplier?); the founder decides.

## 3. Positioning statement (draft)

> For Kigali buyers who need dependable local fruit and goat meat and are tired of unclear quality and unknown sources, **[FARM NAME]** is a small, technology-aware farm in Rwanda that grows to a written specification, records every lot, and shows its work. Unlike trader chains, where the grower is unknown, a [FARM NAME] lot can be traced to a block and a harvest date.

Conditions: the traceability sentence is a **promise for when lots exist**, not a present claim. Until then the website uses the version in §3.1.

### 3.1 Version for today (pre-harvest)

> [FARM NAME] is a farm being developed in Rwanda, with its research, numbers and progress published openly. We are looking for buyers and partners who want to see how a small farm becomes reliable, step by step.

## 4. Mission (draft)

To grow fruit and raise goats in Rwanda that buyers can trust, by testing every decision against evidence, recording what we do, and treating soil, water and neighbours with care.

## 5. Vision (draft)

A small integrated farm that proves, with published numbers, that a few hectares near Kigali can be commercially viable and gentle on the land, and that others can copy.

Note: "proves" is a target. Today the model shows the opposite in the base case (funding gap RWF 6.8M-52.8M, negative 10-year NPV) [E]. The vision statement must not be used in funding materials without that context (see `../investment/pitch-deck.md`).

## 6. Brand personality

| Trait | Means | Does not mean |
|---|---|---|
| Plain-spoken | Short sentences, numbers with their units and labels | Marketing superlatives |
| Careful | Says "estimate" and "unknown" out loud | Hedging everything |
| Patient | Trees take years; says so | Over-promising speed |
| Practical | A tool, a record, a delivery note | Gadget-led technology talk |
| Respectful | Of buyers' time, neighbours, workers, animals | Sentimental farm imagery |

## 7. Taglines (options; all are claims-safe for today unless noted)

| # | English | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Grown with records." | Safe once records exist; today it describes the method, still a plan |
| 2 | "A farm you can follow." | Fits the journal and open progress; safe now |
| 3 | "Fruit and goats, written down." | Quirky; communicates traceability |
| 4 | "Small farm. Open numbers." | Safe now |
| 5 | "Trees, goats, and the evidence." | Safe now; slightly dry |

Avoid: "Pure", "100% natural", "Organic", "Farm to fork", "Best in Rwanda", "Fresh from the farm" (until true). Kinyarwanda and French taglines: written by a native speaker after the English is chosen [U].

## 8. Core customer promise (conditional wording)

What the farm will promise a buyer, **once supply exists** (each line is a commitment the farm must be able to keep; if it cannot, it is not promised):
1. We deliver what the specification sheet says, or we credit it.
2. We tell you before a delay, not after.
3. Every lot has a code you can quote back to us.
4. We answer a complaint within 24 hours and fix it within 7 days (`../go-to-market/sales-plan.md` §8).
5. We tell you what we cannot supply.

Not promised: price below the market, year-round supply, export grade, certificates, organic status.

## 9. Claims ladder (what may be said, and when)

| Claim | Allowed when | Evidence kept |
|---|---|---|
| "We grow [crop]" | Plants are in the ground | Dated photo, planting record |
| "We sell [product]" | A delivery has been made | Delivery note, invoice |
| "Our customers include X" | X agreed in writing to be named | Written permission |
| "Traceable" | Lot codes exist and have been used on deliveries | Lot records |
| "Natural farming" (or similar) | Practices are listed publicly (no synthetic pesticides? no herbicides? which fertiliser?) and are actually in use | Input log, receipts, photos |
| "Organic" | Never, unless a recognised certification is held and shown | Certificate |
| "Sustainable" | Measured indicators published (soil cover, water use, fodder balance) | Records |
| "Export" | An exporter has taken the farm's fruit under a written agreement | Contract |
| "Certified" / "GlobalG.A.P." | Certificate in hand | Certificate |
| "Award-winning", "trusted by" | Never invent | |

Note on inputs: subsidised fertiliser and farm chemicals may be used [V CRP-46]; the brand must not imply otherwise if they are.

## 10. Visual identity direction

Principles: legible in sunlight on a phone, printable in one colour on a delivery note, calm, earthy, and honest about being a working farm.

**Logo.** Wordmark first, set in plain type; optional simple mark (a tree-and-hoof, a leaf-and-lines) drawn later by a designer. It must work as a 1-colour stamp on crates and as a favicon. No fake seals, no certification-style badges.

**Colour (proposal, to test for contrast; hex are starting points [A]).**

| Role | Name | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ink | Soil dark | #2B2A26 | Text |
| Base | Chalk | #F7F5EF | Backgrounds |
| Primary | Leaf | #3F6B3A | Buttons, links |
| Accent | Lake | #2E6F8E | Water, information |
| Warm | Clay | #B5683A | Highlights, goat/livestock sections |
| Signal | Ripe | #D9A21B | Warnings, labels |

Checks: all text/background pairs at least WCAG AA 4.5:1; dark-mode variants defined; never use colour alone to carry meaning.

**Typography.** One humanist sans for text and headings (the repo already loads Geist; a variable sans with full Latin coverage is enough); a mono face for lot codes and numbers. Include a Kinyarwanda-safe character set (standard Latin with diacritics). Right-to-left support is not required: the target languages are Kinyarwanda, English and possibly French.

**Layout and print.** Delivery note, label and crate stamp share one grid: farm name, product, lot code, harvest date, net weight. Use real numbers.

**Icons.** Line icons (lucide, already in the stack) for product and process; no stock illustrations of cartoon farmers.

## 11. Photography and imagery direction

1. **Real photographs only.** Anything shown as "the farm" must be a photograph of the actual farm taken by the team. No stock photos presented as the property; no AI-generated images of the land, crops or animals presented as real [CLAUDE.md rule, brief].
2. **Until photos exist, use clearly labelled placeholders**: a flat grey or soil-coloured panel with the text "Photo to come - [subject]" and the date. Diagrams, maps drawn from the study and charts are acceptable and are labelled "illustration".
3. **Shot list (when the site is accessible):** the gate and access road; the land from the four corners at the same time of day (baseline); the lake edge and the buffer line (legal context); soil pits and sampling; planting day with dated stakes; first leaf; crates with lot codes; the scale at weighing; the delivery note signed; the goat shed when it exists; workers at tasks (with consent).
4. **Style:** natural light, early morning and late afternoon, wide enough to show context, close enough to show texture; a ruler, scale or dated sign in some frames; no heavy filters; no staged smiling.
5. **People:** written consent from anyone identifiable; no children without a guardian's consent; credit workers fairly.
6. **Captions carry facts:** date, place (only after the founder confirms the location), subject, and who took it.
7. **Before/after and progress:** repeat frames from the same point every month; the website's journal shows them.
8. **Animals:** photograph at the shed or paddock, never during procedures that could distress; follow the vet's advice on photographing sick animals.
9. **Metadata:** strip GPS from images before publishing if the founder wants to keep the exact location private.

## 12. Tone of voice

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| "We planted 40 trees on 2027-03-20." (once true) | "Thousands of trees thriving." |
| "Estimated yield: 2-8 million RWF per hectare at maturity; we do not know yet." | "Profitable from year one." |
| "Out of stock until [date]." | "Always fresh, always available." |
| Number + unit + label (V/E/A/U) in documents; plain footnotes on the website | Undefined jargon ("synergies", "smart-farming ecosystem") |
| Short sentences. Active voice. Say what, when, how many. | Superlatives, hype, exclamation marks |
| Kinyarwanda and English side by side where the audience needs it; French only if buyers ask | Machine-translated copy sent without a native check |

Greetings and sign-offs: simple and courteous; *Muraho* and *Murakoze* are fine when the speaker is comfortable. Always give a person's name and a way to reach them.

Writing for buyers: lead with the spec, price basis, delivery days and payment days. Writing for partners and investors: lead with what is known, then estimates, then targets, then unknowns.
