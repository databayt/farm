# Integrated farm design — how the parts fit together

Status: Stage 4 design · Date: 2026-10-19 · Author: farm designer (agronomy, livestock, regenerative design)
Location dependency: **High.** The location is unconfirmed [U]. This is a conditional design by candidate zone (A Rumira/Gashora lowland, B Muhazi shore, C Mugesera, D Shyira highland). Nothing here is a statement about a parcel. Every number is labelled; `[A]` numbers are placeholders to be replaced by a site visit, soil and water tests, and local quotes.
Companion files: `crop-plan.md`, `livestock-plan.md`, `feed-and-pasture-plan.md`, `water-and-soil-plan.md`. New sources are `DSN-nn` in `../registers/incoming/dsn-sources.csv`; everything else reuses existing register IDs.

## 1. The design in one paragraph

A lean, low-input mixed farm of **grafted fruit trees, a small goat herd sized to the feed the land can actually grow, and cut-and-carry fodder**, held together by manure-to-compost, boundary trees that give shade, browse and poles, and a **water budget that sets the orchard size, not the other way round**. Three findings drive everything:

1. **Feed, not pens, limits the herd.** With yields in the range the Rwandan trials support (4–8 t DM/ha/yr pasture [A from LVS-15, LVS-16]), a 5 ha farm carries about 20 breeding does, not the 40–60 in the model's M1–M3 [E, section 4].
2. **Water, not land, limits the orchard.** Supplementary irrigation for a mature avocado/mango/citrus block of 1.5 ha is about 3,500 m³ a year [E, `water-and-soil-plan.md` section 2.2], while 180 m² of roof yields about 123 m³ a year at Gashora, and people and animals already use about 108 m³ of it. The orchard must be phased to proven water, and mango leads where water is unproven.
3. **Overheads dominate at this scale** [E, preliminary report]. So the design avoids optional capital (full-farm drip, sensors, cool store, on-farm nursery), uses live posts and boundary trees instead of bought timber, and keeps one permanent worker.

## 2. How the components support or compete

| Interaction | Supports | Competes | Design response |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Manure → compost → trees** | Goats give nutrients and organic matter for planting holes and mulch; the farm buys less fertiliser (fertiliser subsidy exists but eligibility of a private farm is [U] [CRP-46]) | Supply is small: about 2.4 t DM of collectable manure a year from 20 does [E, section 3]; trees, fodder bank and nursery all want it | **Priority order:** (1) planting holes and mulch for trees; (2) the fodder bank; (3) paddocks. Buy nothing to cover the gap until tests say so |
| **Browse vs seedlings** | Fodder trees (Calliandra, Gliricidia; Leucaena in the lowlands) cut the protein bought in [LVS-21, LVS-22, LVS-23] | Goats strip bark and young trees; browsing is the most likely cause of orchard failure [A, zoning-concept rule 2; no East African tree-protection rule found, CRP open question 8] | Orchard blocks are **fenced out of all grazing**; browse is cut and carried, never grazed in place until stems are above goat reach |
| **Shade vs pasture** | Boundary rows give the animals shade and a windbreak; poles are a long-term asset | Shade lowers grass growth under and beside the row; Eucalyptus suppressed crop yield 80% within 10.5 m in a Rwandan study [CRP-41] | One row of Grevillea/Markhamia at 4 m spacing, on the outside of the fence, not a woodlot; no Eucalyptus beside crops or near the lake |
| **Water: orchard vs fodder vs animals vs people** | Roofs and tanks serve animals and people well | Animals need about 38 m³ a year and people about 55 m³ [E, water plan section 2.1]; the orchard needs 1,000 m³ by year 3 and about 3,500 m³ when mature (25–90 times the animals' demand). Napier irrigated through the four dry months would need about 3,900 m³/ha [E] | **Water goes to trees. Fodder is rain-fed**; the dry season is bridged by hay and silage, not by irrigating grass |
| **Fodder bank vs orchard land** | Cut-and-carry gives 2.7 times more usable DM per ha than grazing in the mid case (9.6 vs 3.6 t) [E, feed plan] and lowers worm pick-up from the ground [A] | Every fodder hectare is a hectare not in fruit (mature avocado grosses about RWF 2.1M/ha at 156 trees [E, CRP-16, CRP-45 at 50%]) | Fodder share of Z3 set at 40%, raised only if records show parasite or feed problems |
| **Goat income vs orchard income** | Goats pay from year 2; trees pay from year 4–6 [A] | At 20 does the herd grosses about RWF 1.0M a year [E, livestock plan section 9]; a mature hectare of avocado grosses about 2 times that | Keep the herd small; do not let goats displace orchard hectares |
| **Cover crops / mulch vs livestock feed** | Lablab and cut grass feed both soil and goats | The same cut grass cannot be both mulch and hay | Mulch first for the tree rows in the dry season; hay from surplus wet-season growth only |
| **Biosecurity vs open markets** | A closed herd kept on the farm is safer than buying market animals | A closed small herd limits genetic choice and sales channels | Buy from few closed herds; sell direct (farm gate, hotels, abattoir buyers) where possible; keep a 2–4 month buffer for closed markets [E, LVS-05 episode] |
| **Fruit food safety vs manure** | Compost is the fertiliser | Raw manure near harvested fruit is a food-safety risk | Compost hot (55 C or more for 3 days, turned) before use on fruit trees [DSN-08, DSN-09 international] |

## 3. Nutrient and manure flows (method and numbers)

Method [E]: faecal DM per animal per day = intake × (1 − digestibility). Digestibility 55% [A, no Rwandan figure]. Share of day housed or fed at the house 50% [A], because cut-and-carry feeding and night housing bring manure to one place.

- Intake per doe unit (doe + 1/25 buck + 0.94 young on hand): 534 kg DM/yr (see feed plan section 2).
- Faecal DM = 534 × 0.45 = 240 kg/yr per doe unit.
- Collectable (50%) = 120 kg DM/yr per doe unit.
- 20 does: 20 × 120 = 2,400 kg DM/yr, about 2.4 t [E]. With bedding and crop residues, compost output is perhaps 3–4 t a year [A].

Demand [A]: tree planting holes and mulch at 10 kg compost per tree per year × about 230 trees (zone A, 5 ha, section 5) = 2.3 t; fodder bank at even 3 t/ha would take 2.9 t. **Supply covers the trees and about a third of the fodder bank.** The balance is closed by (a) legume fodder (Calliandra, Gliricidia, lablab), (b) leaving paddock dung where it falls, and (c) purchased fertiliser only on soil-test evidence.

Compost method: pad ≥ 200 m from the lake bank (see rule P3); C:N 25–30:1, moisture 40–65% [DSN-08]; ≥ 55 C for 3 days with turning [DSN-08, DSN-09]; keep dry-season piles shaded and covered. Test the finished compost (N, P, K, EC) before dosing. The one goat-dung compost analysis found (3.1% N, 3.5% P, 4.5% K) is from Malaysia and looks unusually rich, so it is not used for dosing.

## 4. Protective rules

Legal rules are `[V]`; design rules are `[A]` until a vet or agronomist confirms them.

| ID | Rule | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | No farm or livestock installation within **50 m** of a lake bank, measured from the furthest flood line | [V LOC-01, LOC-02] |
| P2 | No building within **20 m** of a swamp boundary (Zone A is ringed by declared swamps) | [V LOC-02; LOC-46] |
| P3 | **Goat house, kraal, quarantine pen, manure and compost pad at least 200 m from the lake bank.** The law names kraals and slaughterhouses at 200 m; the design applies the same distance to the house and the compost pad as the safe reading | [V LOC-02 for kraal; A for the extension] |
| P4 | No grazing, browsing or tethering inside the 50 m lake strip or the swamp buffer. Animals drink from troughs, never from the lake | [A; lake access by animals is not addressed in the texts, LOC question 4.2] |

Zones in the table are the candidate zones A–D; the land-use zones Z0–Z5 are from `../discovery/zoning-concept.md`. Shares are of the total area; the lake/swamp buffer share Z5 is [U] and set at 10% as in the existing model.

Why the zones differ [E]:
- **A** has the lowest rainfall (927 mm; 8 of 30 years below 800 mm) and no dependable lake water [LOC-34, LOC-46], so the orchard is capped by water and a larger share goes to drought-hardy fodder.
- **B and C** have 950–1,030 mm and a kinder avocado climate, so the orchard gets 35%.
- **D** has 1,190–1,230 mm [LOC-34], higher forage yields and sheep demand, but a cool-climate orchard with unproven buyers, so the orchard stays at 30%.

## 6. Recommended phasing

Month numbers follow the financial model (month 1 = first month of spending). The model's November 2026 start is already unrealistic because the location is unconfirmed; the **sequence and gates are what matter, the calendar slides with the founder's answers.**

| Phase | Window | What happens | Gate to move on |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 Confirm | before month 1 | Pin or UPI; title type and term; survey; lake flood line; 50/200/20 m lines on a base map; soil and water tests (SOIL_N 8, WATER_N 3); P15 go/no-go; vet and agronomist visit | Title safe for long-cycle trees (AS-03); water source identified; P15 passed or herd dropped |
| 1 Secure and prepare | months 1–4 | Perimeter fence on live posts; roof gutters, 160–180 m² of roof and a 30 m³ tank; hardening bed; compost pad; boundary tree row (Z4); contour lines marked; fodder trial plots; order planting material | Fence closed; water store holds the dry-season demand for people and animals (a 30 m³ tank filled from 160–180 m² of roof, water plan 3.1) |
| 2 Feed first | months 4–9 | Plant fodder bank and paddocks in the March–May rains; cut hay as soon as stands allow | First cuts measured; yield per ha recorded |
| 3 Orchard block 1 | the next planting window (March–April or October–November) | Plant **0.5–0.75 ha** only: avocado plus mango (zone-dependent), with drip on the avocado part; guards and fencing | Survival at 12 months ≥ 85% [A]; water meter shows the real dry-season use |
| 4 Goats, tranche 1 | not before month 12; **10 does + 1 buck** | Only when every gate in livestock-plan section 3 is met (RVF, house, quarantine pen, forage, vet, water) | Quarantine complete; kidding and mortality recorded |
| 5 Orchard block 2 | year 2–3 | Remaining orchard area, sized to the measured water supply (borehole or pond if it passed the permit and cost test) | Water gate G-W of the water plan passed (pump test ≥ 3 m³/h, or a permitted pond, or a scheme connection) |
| 6 Goats, tranche 2 and growth | month 24 onward | +8 does and +1 buck; retained female kids bring the herd to the target | Feed balance positive in the dry season; kidding rate and mortality at or better than the planning values |
| 7 Review | end of year 3 | Re-run the model with measured yields, weights, survival and water use; decide on more land, passion fruit/tree tomato (zone D), or sheep | Founder decision |

Decisions that would move the schedule: an RVF market closure or movement ban (hold livestock purchase, keep building feed); an unconfirmed title (hold all tree planting); a failed water test (hold block 2).

## 7. What the design deliberately leaves out [E]

- On-farm tree nursery beyond a hardening bed (savings of about RWF 0.1–0.3M do not pay for the labour and water [CRP supplier file section 5]).
- Sensor networks, cool store, packhouse, M3-level technology: no modelled yield or price benefit exists to justify them.
- Dairy goats (no milk market [LVS-01]), macadamia, cooking banana as a cash crop, guava.
- Irrigating the whole farm. Irrigation is for trees in their first three dry seasons and then for avocado and citrus only.

## 8. Open items that would change this design

1. Location, title and the 50/200/20 m lines (P15).
2. Water: a borehole result, a pond permit decision, or a scheme connection.
3. Whether a private or company-held farm gets PSAC seedlings, the fertiliser subsidy, SSIT irrigation subsidy or subsidised lime [CRP-12, CRP-46, CRP-35, DSN-11] (all [U]).
4. A Rwandan vet's view on RVF/PPR vaccination, deworming strategy and quarantine length.
5. Measured forage yield per hectare (replaces the 4–8 t DM/ha range).
6. Rwandan avocado yield by age at a mature orchard (the 2–8 M RWF/ha spread, AS-05).
