Status: Stage 4 design · Date: 2026-10-19
Location dependency: **High.** Conditional by candidate zone. Evidence labels: [V] verified (source ID), [E] estimate (method shown), [A] assumption, [U] unknown. International figures are named as international.

## 1. Decisions in brief

| Decision | Choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Species | **Goats** in zones A, B, C. Goats plus a small sheep flock only in D | Bugesera has about 20 times more goat than sheep households [V LOC-24]; goat is 19% of national meat output vs 5% for sheep [V LVS-02]; mutton "attracts the fewest customers" [V LVS-07]; Haemonchus infects more sheep (83%) than goats (72%) [V LVS-46] |
| Breed | **Local Small East African does** from named closed herds; one improved buck (Boer or Galla type) tried only after year 1, on a few does | 97% of Bugesera goats are local [V LVS-10]; exotic and crossbred goats get ill more often (37.5% vs 27.6%) [V LVS-01]; no Rwandan performance data for Boer crosses [U]; no breeder with public stock and prices found [U LVS suppliers] |
| Model | **Meat first** (finish kids for Kigali), with a small recorded nucleus; breeding-stock sales only after 2–3 years of records | Demand and price evidence is for meat [V LVS-01, LVS-02]; breeding-stock demand exists but needs proven, healthy, recorded animals [V LVS-01]; only 4% of Bugesera farms keep records [V LVS-10] |
| Size | **20 breeding does at 5 ha** (8 at 2 ha, 40 at 10 ha; 18 and 36 for zones B and C at 5 and 10 ha, 7 at 2 ha) | Set by the dry-matter balance at 75% of mid-case capacity, feed plan section 4 [E] |
| Timing | First purchase **not before model month 12**, only through the gates in section 3 | RVF quarantine; month 9 (July) is the driest month, before any forage has been measured [E, LOC-34] |
| Insurance | None available; self-insure by staging and a feed reserve | The national scheme does not cover goats or sheep [V MKT-21] |

## 2. Breed choice and genetics

| Option | Evidence | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Local Small East African goat | 76% of goats nationally, 97% in Bugesera; adults 20–25 kg; hardy, fertile, some parasite resistance [V LVS-01, LVS-10] | **Base herd** |
| Boer or Boer cross | 2.5% pure Boer and 7.9% crosses nationally; breed descriptions 80–135 kg are descriptive, not Rwandan trial data; more disease and higher treatment need [V LVS-01] | Trial: one buck on a small group after 12 months, with weights recorded |
| Galla | 0.3% of goats; 45–55 kg females [V LVS-01] | Same trial option |
| Saanen (dairy) | No goat-milk collection or processing in Rwanda [V LVS-01] | No |
| Sheep (D only) | Local fat-tailed hair sheep; Merino-type crosses suit medium and high rainfall; RAB Gishwati multiplies Merino for approved farmers (60 of 80 applications approved) [V LVS-01, LVS-09, LVS-56]; Dorper: not found [U] | Zone D: 8–10 ewes, from the RAB application or local closed flocks |

Where to buy [E from LVS suppliers file]: local does from **several small, dispersed, closed herds** found through the sector veterinary officer and cooperatives; bucks from a healthy, recorded source. Market animals are the volume source but have unknown origin and health, and PPR entered Rwanda with roughly 300 goats of unknown origin [V LVS-14]. RAB Nyagatare is described as a source of improved goat genetics but no stock list or price exists [V LVS-01, LVS-50; U]. Verify with RAB and the district vet before relying on it.

Mating policy [A]: one buck per 20 does or fewer (model RATIO 25 is allowed for mature, fertile bucks, but 20 is safer with local stock); never mate a buck with his daughters, so keep two bucks once the first daughters mature (replace one every 2 years from another herd after quarantine); castrate surplus male kids early (as Bugesera farmers do before 3 months [V LVS-10]) except those selected as bucks, and keep entire bucks away from does outside the mating windows.

## 4. Staged herd introduction

Female : male ratio is 20 : 1 or fewer, with a second buck added at the second tranche.

| Stage | Timing (model month) | Purchase | Herd after | Condition to go on |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| T1 | ≥ 12 and all gates met | **10 breeding does + 1 buck**, 12–24 months old, from 2–3 closed herds | 10 does + 1 buck | Quarantine complete; no unexplained deaths in the first 3 months; supplier scored (livestock suppliers file section 5) |
| Hold | months 12–24 | None; kids born from T1 (about 15.6 a year) | 12–13 does by month 24 (retention 80% of female kids) | Pre-weaning mortality ≤ 20% [A]; adult mortality ≤ 8% [A]; feed balance positive in the dry season |
| T2 | ≥ 24 | **+6 does + 1 buck** | about 19–20 does + 2 bucks | Same gates re-checked (RVF, feed, vet) |
| Growth | months 24–48 | Home-bred replacements only; retention falls from 80% to 50% as the target is reached | **20 does + 2 bucks** (target) | Raise the target to 25 only after two dry seasons with a measured forage surplus (feed plan 5) |

Herd growth check [E]: N(t+1) = N(t) × (1 − adult loss 0.08 − cull 0.15) + retained female kids; retained female kids per doe = 1.2 births × 1.3 kids × 0.80 survive × 0.5 female × 0.8 retained = 0.50. So N grows by 27% a year (0.77 + 0.50 = 1.27). From 10 does: 12.7 (year 1), 16.1 (year 2), 20.4 (year 3) without any second purchase; the T2 purchase of 6 shortens that by about a year. [A inputs; no Rwandan performance data, LVS-59 holds only a 1988 abstract without figures.]

### 4.2 Sheep in zone D [A]

Introduce from month 18 at the earliest, 8 ewes + 1 ram, only after the goat herd has run one season; sheep carry more worms than goats [V LVS-46], goat mineral mixes can be toxic to sheep because of copper [V LVS-26], and sheep are more exposed to saponins in pure *Brachiaria decumbens* stands [V LVS-43]. Keep sheep and goats in separate groups and paddocks. DRC buyers pay about RWF 100,000 for a 30–35 kg Nyabihu sheep (one farmer, 2024) [V LVS-56]; that is one quote, not a market study.

## 5. Housing

All distances and sizes are [A] pending the survey and a vet or animal-housing adviser.

- **Site**: ≥ 200 m from the lake bank (rule P3), ≥ 20 m from swamp, on dry, slightly raised ground with drainage away from the water point, tank and orchard; downwind of the house if possible. Not on the best orchard soil.
- **Type**: a raised slatted floor (about 1 m above ground, timber or bamboo), open sides with a roof of sheeting that also feeds the rainwater tank, long axis east–west. Manure drops through to a collection floor and is carried to the compost pad.
- **Space**: adults 1.2 m² each, kid area 0.3 m² each [A]. For 20 does + 2 bucks + 19 young on hand: 26.4 + 5.7 = **32 m²**; use **33 m²** as the model's 20 × 1.1 × 1.5 (M2HEAD = 1.5).
- **Cost** [A, model HOUSE RWF 45,000/m²]: 33 m² × 45,000 = **RWF 1.5M**. For comparison the existing M2 inputs give (50 + 20) × 1.1 × 1.2 = 92 m², about RWF 4.2M.
- **Fittings**: raised feed racks (also keeps feed off the floor, which lowers worm pick-up), water trough with a float, a kidding pen, a sick pen, a hoof-trimming and weighing crate, a lockable feed store (model FEEDSTORE RWF 0.8M, M2).
- **Quarantine pen**: separate, at least 30 m from the main house [A], on its own drainage, with a 2 m gap (double fence) from any neighbour's animals and its own tools, boots and water; fed last.
- **Security**: theft is a named risk for small ruminants [V LVS-01]: lockable house, lighting on the solar kit, a dog or a night watch, tags and photos.

## 6. Biosecurity and quarantine

1. **Closed herd.** Buy only from named closed herds; breed your own replacements.
2. **Screen the origin** before the vet visits: district, sector, recent notifiable-disease reports, abortions in the flock [LVS suppliers file section 5].
3. **Paperwork** every time: movement certificate; keep copies [V LVS-29].
4. **Quarantine on arrival**: separate pen, water and feed; no contact with the resident herd or cattle; daily observations written down. **Length, tests and treatments are set by the farm's vet.**
5. **No cattle contact**: mixing cattle and goats was the strongest brucellosis risk factor in Nyagatare [V LVS-39].
6. **Boundary**: double fence or ≥ 2 m gap against neighbours' animals; no sharing of bucks, water points or grazing.
7. **Visitors and vehicles** stay out of the animal area; feed the quarantine group last; clean footwear.
8. **Report** suspected notifiable disease immediately (RVF signs: high fever, weakness, bleeding from body openings) [V LVS-03, LVS-40]. RVF is also a human zoonosis; wear gloves when handling aborted material and birth fluids [V LVS-34].
9. **Dead animals**: report unexplained deaths to the vet; bury or compost them away from water and ≥ 200 m from the lake.
10. **Biosecurity budget**: the model's QUAR RWF 0.6M is enough for a pen and race on 5 ha.

## 7. Welfare

- **Water and feed** available every day; dry-matter intake planning at 2.5–3.5% of body weight, higher for late-pregnant and lactating does [V LVS-26, LVS-27].
- **Shade and dry floor**; no tethering at the orchard; space as in section 5.
- **Kids**: colostrum within hours of birth, kept with the dam, weaned at about 3 months [A], with a clean kidding pen.
- **Health**: a vet relationship, treatment records, culling of chronic cases rather than dosing them indefinitely.
- **Procedures** (castration, disbudding, hoof trim): by the vet or a trained person with pain control the vet recommends.
- **Transport and slaughter**: short trips, shade and water, no overcrowding, avoid the hottest hours; slaughter only in a RICA-licensed premise [V LVS-28]. Traders reported they may not move animals at night [V LVS-01].
- **Heat and dry-season stress**: hay reserve and troughs before animals go thin, not after.

## 8. Health programme framework (confirm every row with a Rwandan veterinarian)

| Issue | Evidence | Farm action (non-drug) | Ask the vet |
|---|---|---|---|
| **RVF** | Outbreaks in 2022, 2024 and 2026; whole Eastern Province quarantined from 2026-06-26; free vaccination at village sites in 2026 [V LVS-03, LVS-04, LVS-11] | Respect movement bans; hold stock; keep records; avoid contact with aborted material | Which vaccine, which species, when, and whether a disease-free farm may sell direct to licensed abattoirs during a quarantine |
| **PPR** | Declared Sept 2023, still endemic May 2024; FAO advises coverage above 70%; 13.6% of sampled goats had antibodies (Bugesera and others) [V LVS-14, LVS-38, LVS-45] | Closed herd, quarantine | Vaccination plan for kids and does; RAB sells vaccines through IremboGov to vets [V LVS-44] |
| **Gastrointestinal worms (Haemonchus)** | Top health problem in Bugesera (58% of farms), deaths peak in April–May; 71.8% of Nyagatare goats infected, peaks at the two rainy seasons, worst in low swampy sectors; authors suggest FAMACHA-guided targeted treatment [V LVS-10, LVS-46] | Cut-and-carry feeding off the floor, rotational grazing with long rests, no grazing on wet low ground, raised feeders, selecting animals that stay healthy, FAMACHA scoring by a trained person | Which drug classes work locally (resistance), when to test, FAMACHA training, faecal egg counts at a lab |
| **Brucellosis** | 6–11% animal-level and 16.6% of herds positive in Nyagatare goats [V LVS-39] | No cattle mixing; reject animals with abortion history | Test breeding animals before purchase; human-health advice for handlers |
| **Orf, pneumonia, CCPP, foot rot, ticks, heartwater** | Orf on 17–33% of Bugesera farms; others listed without Rwandan prevalence [V LVS-10, LVS-01; U] | Dry floors, trimming, tick checks, ventilation | Local risk and what to vaccinate or treat |
| **Minerals** | Goat mixes are toxic to sheep (copper) [V LVS-26] | Separate goat and sheep mineral supply | Which mineral for the local soils |
| **Plant toxins** | Suspected saponin poisoning in Boer goats fed *Brachiaria* 'Mulato' hay [V LVS-25]; mimosine in Leucaena above 30% of the diet [V LVS-22] | Keep any one forage below half of the diet [A]; vary | Safe proportions |
| **Abortion and unexplained deaths** | Disease reporting duty [V LVS-40] | Keep the aborted material, isolate, wear gloves | Diagnosis through the district vet or the lab network [V LVS-08] |

Vet access: 6% of surveyed farmers said access was low; there are 15 proximity veterinary posts and 5 laboratories (Rubirizi plus Nyagatare, Musanze, Rubavu, Huye) [V LVS-01, LVS-08]; retail vet pharmacies near the zones were not found [U]. Model VET RWF 6,000 and MINERAL 2,500 per head per year are placeholders [A].

## 10. Reproduction calendar and sales schedule

Gestation is about 150 days (149–152 in several studies; international breeds) and kidding intervals 276–337 days in the literature [DSN-10]. So 1.2 births a year is a realistic planning value for a managed local herd [A], not a proven Rwandan one.

| Window | Mating | Births | Sale at 9 months | Market |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **W1 (70% of does)** | September–October | February–March | November–December | Christmas and New Year: Saban slaughter rises from about 150 to 600 goats a day and the abattoir price about 25% [V LVS-07] |
| W2 (30% of does) | March–April | August–September | May–June | Eid al-Adha: about 2027-05-16/17 and about 2028-05-05 [E from LVS-35, LVS-36, lunar calendar]; but late pregnancy and lactation fall in the dry season, so W2 needs the hay reserve [V LVS-26] |

Other sales: culls with W1; surplus bucks as meat; **breeding stock from year 3** at a premium (model BPREM 0.3 base) once records, brucellosis tests and vaccination history exist. Routes: farm gate to Saban traders and licensed abattoir buyers, hotels and bars directly (hospitality is the largest buyer [V LVS-01]), DRC traders in the west (zone D) [V LVS-56]. No buyer has been contacted; a buyer is not a customer until a commitment exists. In a market closure keep the animals and feed them from the reserve (G9).

### 10.1 Value at the target herd (20 does) [E]

- Births: 20 × 1.2 × 1.3 = 31.2 a year; weaned 31.2 × 0.80 = 25.0.
- Replacement does needed: 20 × (0.08 + 0.15) = 4.6; sold kids 25.0 − 4.6 = **20.4**.
- Kid revenue: 20.4 × 18 kg × RWF 2,300 = **RWF 0.84M**; cull does (3) at 28 kg × 2,300 = RWF 0.19M. **Gross about RWF 1.0M a year.**
- Vet and minerals (RWF 8,500 per head × about 41 head) = RWF 0.35M; transport RWF 2,000 × 23 = 0.05M; some bought feed. **Margin before labour and depreciation about RWF 0.4–0.6M.**
- The 2022 Bugesera price of a medium animal was RWF 40,000 [V LVS-01], and 18 kg × 2,300 = RWF 41,400 agrees with it [E].
- **The herd is small money relative to RWF ~3M of overheads.** Its job is early cash, manure for the trees, and a platform that scales at 10 ha (40 does = RWF 2M gross). That is the honest reading of the "overheads vs scale" problem.
