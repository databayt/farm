# Speaker notes — pitch deck

Status: Stage 6 draft · Date: 2026-10-19 · Companion to `pitch-deck.md` (20 slides; about 15 minutes plus questions)
Stance for the whole talk: say what is known, what is estimated, what is a target and what is unknown. If asked for something that is not in the study, say "unknown, and here is how we find out". Never round a prospect up to a customer. Never speak a figure that has no label.

## Before you present

- Confirm who is in the room and why. This is a conversation about a farm at the study stage, not a raise.
- Check the numbers against the latest model run; if quotes have arrived (T17), update slides 12-13 first.
- Bring the register extract: 306 sources, buyer register (53 prospects, 0 contacted unless updated), price sheet.
- Do not show any photo unless it is a real photograph taken at the farm; placeholders stay labelled.

## Slide notes

**1. Title and thesis (30 s).** "This is a farm in the study stage. I will show you what we know, what we estimate, what we aim for and what we do not know. We are not asking for money today." Thesis: selling directly to Kigali buyers, with records, may earn better than farm-gate prices. Say "may". If asked "why believe it?": we do not yet; slide 10 shows how we test it.

**2. Location and assets (1 min).** The founder's land is an existing 5 ha (range 2-10) near a lake in the broader Kigali region; capital about RWF 35M (under USD 25k). Say plainly that the exact location, title, and water are not yet confirmed and are the first tasks. Describe the candidate zones as candidates. Point to the lake rules: they are law, they reduce usable land and decide where the goat shed can go. If asked about title: "Foreign holders get leases up to 99 years, not freehold [Law 27/2021]; we will read the actual title before planting trees."

**3. Market opportunity (1.5 min).** Walk through the e-Soko table: avocado 600/700/800, passion fruit 1,800/2,000/2,400, goat meat 7,250/8,000/8,500 (farm-gate/wholesale/retail, RWF/kg, national 30-day medians to 14 October 2026). Avocado exports have grown about five-fold in five years (from about 840 t to over 4,200 t). Inflation is high (urban +17.2%, rural +22%), so prices and costs move quickly. Two cautions: e-Soko "farm-gate" is probably near-market, so a remote farm gets less; and global avocado oversupply in 2025-26 depressed early-season prices. If asked "is there demand for what you will grow?": "The market is real; whether the farm can get a good price from it is exactly what the next 8 weeks of interviews will tell us."

**4. Customer needs (1 min).** Competitors say they compete on reliability, consistency and fast payment. We have not spoken to a single buyer yet. State that. The interview guide, price sheet and qualification criteria are ready. Gate: 15 interviews, 4 weeks of price data, 2 written intents by early December.

**5. Target products (1.5 min).** Avocado: strongest story, slowest; yield is the biggest uncertainty (RWF 2-8M per hectare at maturity). Passion fruit: fastest, but virus-prone; need certified material. Goats: real demand but disease rules (RVF) can close markets for months. Be clear that none is confirmed; each needs a buyer first.

**6. Competitive landscape (1 min).** Do not disparage. The market is full of exporters and aggregators who already run outgrower schemes. A 5 ha farm is an outgrower by size. "Our edge, if any, is being small, reliable and open. We have not proven it."

**7. Integrated model (1 min).** Trees, goats and fodder on one site. The diagram is an illustration, not a survey. The three models differ mainly in how much is invested; the technology-heavy one costs more and has no evidenced extra revenue so far.

**8. Natural farming (45 s).** Say what we intend to do and that we will log it. Say what we do not claim: no "organic", no certification. We may use subsidised fertiliser and will say so.

**9. Technology (1 min).** The honest answer: records first. Sensors and automation wait for evidence they pay. Mention the model finding: the technology-enabled farm (M3) invests much more and has a much larger gap in the base case.

**10. Go-to-market (1.5 min).** Five phases with gates. First channel to test: direct-collecting aggregators and online grocers, plus exporters' outgrower terms. Goat trade on hold until RVF rules are clear. Pricing policy: reference e-Soko; premium only when earned. Emphasise: we will not plant a crop without a named channel.

**11. Business model (45 s).** Revenue by line, farm-gate pricing in the model. Overheads of about RWF 3M a year are a placeholder and are as large as the revenue from a mature 1.25 ha orchard plus a 40-doe herd. This is the biggest threat and the reason scale matters.

**12. Capital requirements (1 min).** Capital 35M; the gap in the base cases is 6.8M (M1) to 52.8M (M3). Costs are placeholders. "The next step with the highest value is getting real quotes."

**13. Financial projections (1.5 min).** Read the table. Say: "In the base case on 5 ha at farm-gate prices this does not pay back within 10 years. The upside case breaks even around year 8 with IRR 10.7%. We are not presenting a profit forecast." Explain what would change it: yields, price, overheads, land.

**14. Risks (1.5 min).** Choose the top three for the audience: title, RVF, overheads. For each, say what we will do and when. Do not claim a risk is "managed" if the mitigation is only planned.

**15. Timeline (45 s).** Gates G0-G4 in 12 weeks; planting in the March 2027 rains if the gates pass. If the title or site fails, we stop.

**16. Team (45 s).** Founder bio [to be supplied]. Say who is not yet engaged (agronomist, veterinarian). Do not name anyone who has not agreed.

**17. Funding request (1 min).** "We are not raising. Here is why and what would change that." Read the evidence list. If someone offers money: thank them, ask nothing, record it, and explain the gates.

**18. Use of funds (30 s).** Principle only: capital that raises yield, price or volume; not overheads.

**19. Milestones (30 s).** Each is a measurable gate; none has been achieved.

**20. Long-term (45 s).** Expansion follows evidence. The most realistic route is more hectares or aggregating neighbours' produce, because scale fixes the overhead problem.

## Likely questions and straight answers

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Who are your customers? | None yet. 53 prospects identified from public sources; none contacted; interviews planned. |
| What is your traction? | None. We have a study, a model and a plan. |
| What are you raising and at what valuation? | Nothing now. We will revisit after buyers and quotes exist. |
| Is the business profitable? | In the base case on 5 ha at farm-gate prices, no. The upside case reaches about 10.7% IRR. The costs are placeholders. |
| Are you organic? | No claim. We will publish what we do. |
| Can you export? | Export potential, not readiness. A farm of this size would sell to an exporter or aggregator. |
| What about the disease outbreak? | A Rift Valley fever quarantine covered the Eastern Province from 26 June 2026; we treat goat timing as dependent on the rules and keep a 2-4 month buffer. |
| Who owns the land? | The founder says it is his or his company's; the title is not yet verified. |
| What if you cannot get good prices? | The model's downside tells us; we would shrink the farm, change the mix or stop. |
| Do you have partners or government support? | None signed. Some programmes exist; eligibility is unknown; the main matching-grant window closed on 27 February 2026. |

## Words to avoid

"Guaranteed", "proven", "premium", "organic", "certified", "export-ready", "disruptive", "unique", "partners include", "customers include", "we have secured", "revenue of".
