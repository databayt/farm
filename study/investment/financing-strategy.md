# Financing strategy — is outside capital justified now?

Status: Stage 6 draft · Date: 2026-10-19 · Location, title, costs: **unconfirmed / placeholders**

## 1. Verdict

**No. External capital is not justified now.** The evidence cannot support a raise, and a raise would be priced at the worst possible moment. Recommended path: **bootstrap a validation phase from the founder's own cash (about RWF 0.6-1.3M [A]), commit the larger capital in tranches tied to decision gates, use only low-cost public support the farm proves eligible for, and revisit outside financing at gate C3/C4 with real quotes and real buyers.** If the evidence is poor, the right move is a smaller farm, a different mix or no farm, not more money.

Additional points:
- The founder's own capital (RWF 35M, about USD 23.7k at RWF 1,475.24/USD [E MKT-04], under USD 25k) is already below the base-case peak need for M1 (41.8M), M2 (42.6M) and M3 (87.8M). The gap is a **sizing and cost question**, and the first 90 days are designed to answer it (T12, T17).
- An investor ticket of this size (tens of millions of RWF) is small for institutional funds. The realistic outside providers are family, a partner, a development bank guarantee or suppliers. Each asks for a plan with quotes and buyers that does not exist yet.
- Selling equity before gates C1-C3 would price the farm on a story. The same story will be worth more after a site is mapped, costs are quoted and one buyer has paid.
- Debt on a farm with no revenue for 3-5 years (avocado) needs collateral and servicing from other income. A concession allows mortgage of the concession rights but not of the land [V MKT-07], and bank agricultural lending is thin (about 6% of commercial credit; 17% of farmers have formal finance [V MKT-05]).

| D. Scale-up decision | After C4 | Debt (BRD guarantee via a SACCO or bank), partner, outgrower pre-finance, more land | Sized from the real model | Positive unit economics on the farm's actual numbers |

Rules:
1. No outside money before Stage D unless it is non-dilutive, low-cost and does not tie the farm to a buyer or crop that failed the gates.
2. Never spend a tranche that would leave the farm unable to carry the first 12 months of overheads.
3. If quotes put the full plan above the capital, build a **pilot block** (about 1.25 ha avocado plus a small passion-fruit block, no livestock until RVF is clear) and let sales fund the next block.
4. If a funder offers money before the gates, record the offer in the decision log; take it only if the terms are better than waiting.

## 5. What would make outside capital justified

Raise or borrow only when **all** hold:
1. Location mapped, title confirmed, and the title term covers the tree cycle (G0, T04).
2. Model rerun with real quotes (T17); the funding gap is a stated number, not a placeholder.
3. Gate C2 passed for each planted crop; gate C3 passed for the first sales.
4. The ask is specific: `ask = peak funding need - founder capital + 20% contingency` [A]. On today's placeholders M1 base would be about RWF 8.2M (6.8M x 1.2), M2 about 9.1M, M3 about 63M, and the M1 downside about 72M; **these are illustrations of the formula, not requests**.
5. At least two buyers paid on time and one repeat order exists (C3, C4).
6. The use of funds is capital that raises yield, price or volume by a measured amount (irrigation, planting material, a shed), not overhead.

## 6. Alternative to funding: shrink or reshape

If Stage A shows that quotes and prices keep the base case negative, the strategy is **not** a bigger raise. Options in order: smaller scope (pilot block, no livestock), cheaper overheads (shared labour, no building), cheaper land lease, a higher-value mix (passion fruit and papaya early, avocado later), or stop. The decision log records the choice [D-006 pattern].
