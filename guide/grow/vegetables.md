# Vegetable field: from bare ground to the galley

Status: guide of 2026-10-28. The field, its soil and its water are not yet measured; sizes follow the provisional master plan.

How to start the vegetable field that feeds the galley and sells its surplus: the seasons, the beds, the water, the crops and the first month, step by step.

## The seasons

| Season | Months | What the field does |
|---|---|---|
| A | September to February | Raise seedlings for 3 to 8 weeks, transplant with the rains, drip through the January-February lull |
| B | March to June | Transplant in February and March, raised beds for the April rains, drip again from late May |
| C | July to September | Drip only: about 4 to 5 mm of water a day evaporates; plant only what the proven water can carry |

Seasons [V LOC-53]; rain and evaporation at Gashora [E LOC-34, LOC-54]; nursery times [V CRP-68].

## The first month, step by step (November)

1. **Test the soil.** Take samples from 10 spots across the zone, mix them, and send them to RAB's soil laboratory: pH, organic carbon, nitrogen, phosphorus, texture [V CRP-72].
2. **Test the water.** Measure what the borehole gives over 6 hours, and have a sample tested; the field needs about 2 m³ an hour for 6 hours in the dry months [E].
3. **Fence the zone** against animals and keep one gate on the garden walk to the galley.
4. **Mark out the beds:** 1.2 m beds, 0.5 m paths, 1 m paths between blocks of 10 beds; the beds run across the slope.
5. **Build the first 40 beds:** dig, mix in compost or manure (about 250 kg per 100 m² for tomatoes [V CRP-68]), raise them 20 cm.
6. **Lay the drip:** a header pipe from the tank, a filter, two drip lines per bed; run it for an hour and check every emitter.
7. **Start the nursery:** trays or a seedbed under shade net for kale, cabbage, onion, tomato and sweet pepper.
8. **Sow straight into the beds:** amaranth and French beans, which give a harvest within 4 to 8 weeks.
9. **Add lime only if the test says so:** the vegetables want pH 6.0 to 7.0; the soils here are likely nearer 5.5 to 6.1 [V CRP-68, E LOC-37].

## Water

1. The driest months need 4.1 to 5.3 litres per m² of bed a day: 10 to 13 m³ a day for the whole field [E CRP-75, LOC-54].
2. That is about ten times what the roofs can collect, so the borehole or the mains carries the field; plant season C only to the flow the test proves.
3. Water at dawn or dusk, never at midday; mulch the beds with dry grass to keep the water in.
4. Under 1 ha of irrigation needs no permit from the Water Resources Board [V LOC-08].

## Keeping pests down

1. Rotate: never the same family (tomato and pepper and eggplant; cabbage and kale; beans) in the same bed two seasons running.
2. Walk the beds twice a week; pull and burn any plant that wilts suddenly (bacterial wilt).
3. Hang pheromone traps for Tuta absoluta in the tomato beds.
4. Spray only what the agro-dealer's advisor names for the pest found, and keep a record of every spray.

## Done when

1. The soil and water tests are back and the beds are limed and fed to them.
2. Forty beds are planted, with drip running and no dry emitters.
3. The galley receives a harvest list every morning and the field records every kilogram.
