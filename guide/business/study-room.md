# The study room's business

Status: first model of 2026-11-02 (D-073). On 2026-11-03 the venues, the fit-out's shop prices and the start before the room opens were added. Prices marked [V] are rates other places publish; every price of ours, every booking and every cost is an assumption [A] until a pilot course and the first three months are measured. The research behind it: [the study room's market](../../study/research/study-room-market.md).

What the study room sells, to whom, at what price, and how many days a month it must fill.

## At a glance

| Rank | Line | Who buys | Why it ranks here |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **A course for one company's team**, two days, with a night at the farm | companies, NGOs, startups and boards in Kigali; the factories of the economic zone, 14 minutes away | the only line whose price pays for the hour's drive, and the one a hotel cannot copy: the team's own skills |
| 2 | **A small team's retreat** in the room, with its own agenda | the same buyers, without a course | the same room-day and the same beds and meals, with no trainer to pay |
| 3 | **Funded cohorts and school days**: the room as a programme's venue, and groups of eight from the schools nearby | training programmes and their partners; a girls' science academy, an agriculture university and an IB school within a few kilometres | the biggest money for skills in Rwanda, reached through calls, consortia and tenders, so it comes in the second year; the schools' budgets are not known |
| 4 | **Open courses** sold by the seat | individuals | local courses sell for RWF 30,000–120,000 a person and free ones compete, so at our cost of time an open day loses money: one a quarter, as marketing |
| 5 | **Day passes** | guests already staying | a desk in Kigali costs RWF 8,000–10,000 a day [V MICE-01], and the bus to Gashora and back RWF 5,400–5,800 [E LOC-P08, LOC-P09]; a pass is a weekday guest's extra, never the room's business |

The room's real job is the farm's weekdays. The stays sell mostly weekends to Kigali ([marketing](marketing.md)); the room brings groups from Monday to Thursday, and each group also buys beds, meals and the evening by the fire.

## The room, as sold

- **Eight seats** at one long table, a 65-inch screen with a lectern, Wi-Fi, bookshelves and a reading corner; no air-conditioning, two wall fans ([the room's plan](../study-room/plan.md)).
- **Eight beds** for a group that stays: the farm has five private units (two studios, three guest rooms) and tents; a group of eight sleeps one to a unit plus three tents, or shares [E].
- **Meals** from the galley at the dining tables, and the lounge and the fire pit in the evening.
- **Larger groups** (more than eight) are an event on the lawn, on the [business model](model.md)'s events line, not the room's.

## The place

- **An hour from Kigali:**
  - 58.5 km, about 50 minutes free-flow [E LOC-19];
  - by bus from Nyanza (Kicukiro), RWF 5,400–5,800 there and back with the city bus [E LOC-P08, LOC-P09];
  - the airport expressway has no completion date [V LOC-64].
- **Next door:** the Bugesera economic zone is in Gashora sector, 14 minutes by road. It has 12 factories and about 1,500 workers today, plans 7,500 jobs, and will have its own training institute in a later phase [V LOC-70, LOC-72].
- **The airport,** 20 to 29 km by road, targets 2028. Its planned city has conference hotels and a convention centre for 5,500 [V LOC-57, LOC-58, LOC-73].
- **Few rooms:** the whole district had 77 places to stay and 1,190 rooms in 2024 [V LOC-75], and none publishes a meeting rate [U].
- **Schools within a few kilometres:**
  - Gashora Girls Academy of Science and Technology, about 285 students [V LOC-79];
  - the Rwanda Institute for Conservation Agriculture [V LOC-81];
  - Ntare Louisenlund School, an IB school [V LOC-82];
  - and the free Imbuto Hub in Nyamata [V LOC-85].
- **The plans:** the district wants to be "a regional hub for ... conferences" [V LOC-86], and the country targets USD 224 million a year from meetings by 2029 [V LOC-87].

So [E]: two markets, Kigali's small teams now and Bugesera's employers and schools as they grow, and a head start: the airport targets 2028, and its city's conference hotels come after it. The room competes by being small, quiet and taught, not by its size.

What the groups bring to the rest of the farm, each month [E]: 24 nights (two courses and the retreat, eight people, one night each) at about RWF 30,000 a bed, 24 dinners and breakfasts at 15,000, and 48 lunch days at 15,000: about **RWF 1.8M a month** on the stays' and food lines, part of the 45% occupancy the [business model](model.md) already assumes.

The room's own fixed costs (its share of the internet, upkeep and the screen's replacement, and its marketing) are about RWF 120,000 a month [A AS-36]: less than half a course day covers them. What a month leaves after them, about RWF 1.5M, is what pays back the fit-out.

**The fit-out.** Priced on 2026-11-03 from what Kigali shops publish, the room's furniture, screen and devices come to about **RWF 5.0M to 11.8M** [E, from FIT-P01 to FIT-P37].
- **The low end:** local solid-wood pieces and a 65-inch Sayona screen.
- **The high end:** imported furniture and a Samsung screen.
- **Payback:** about **3 to 8 base months** [E].

What the range leaves out:
- **The lectern,** which no shop prices.
- **The container's own conversion:** the floor, the linings and the electrics, which follow the studio's list.
- **Delivery.**

The long table, 3.6 × 0.75 m, is not sold ready-made: the tables on sale are 2.2 × 1.0 m and 3.2 × 1.1 m. So it is a carpenter's piece [E]. Written quotes come next ([materials](../study-room/materials.md)).

The first year is lower [A AS-35]: one pilot course at half the tuition and one retreat in months 1–3 (about 1.1M); one course, one retreat and the passes a month in months 4–6 (about 1.5M a month); the base month from month 7. That is about RWF 20M from the room in year one.

## Funded seats and school days

The money for skills in Rwanda is large, but it reaches a provider through calls, consortia and tenders, not by the seat:

1. **The Skills Development Fund** (RTB) funds private training providers through competitive, results-based calls; ICT and energy are eligible [U EDU-20, EDU-23]; the World Bank's USD 200m programme pays into it [V EDU-18].
2. **Mastercard Foundation** money flows through consortia (DOT Rwanda, kLab, Igire, SOLVIT Africa), not open calls [V EDU-15].
3. **NGOs, UN agencies and microfinance banks** buy staff training and retreats through requests for proposals, often for 10 to 25 people at a three- or four-star venue [V MICE-32, MICE-34, MICE-35, EDU-38]. One NGO's framework of conference venues already lists Bugesera [V MICE-34].
4. **The schools near the farm:**
   - Gashora Girls Academy runs a Makerspace and has sent its teachers abroad for ICT training [V LOC-79];
   - the agriculture university is open to training partnerships, the district's plan says [V LOC-86].
   - A coding, AI or solar day for a group of eight, and a teachers' course, are their offers. Their budgets are not known [U].

The way in is as the venue or a subcontractor of an existing partner first, then as a provider once the rules allow it.

## The rules that shape it

The research's [rules](../../study/research/study-room-market.md#4-the-rules), in short:

1. **Ask RTB before selling a course.** A company that runs short courses "must submit a request" to RTB, and teaching without the inspection agency's approval can be fined RWF 500,000 to 1,000,000 [V REG-35, REG-34]. Whether a professional workshop counts is not said [U]. Until RTB answers in writing, the room sells closed courses to one company, each with databayt's own certificate of attendance, and never calls a course a diploma.
2. **No venue licence:** the farm's accommodation licence is the one that applies to groups [E, from REG-40, REG-41]. Meals for day guests who do not stay may need a restaurant licence [U]. The fire rules for a "public building" may ask for sprinklers and CCTV in a room this size [U REG-42].
3. **Tax on every quote:** tuition, the room, meals and beds are taxable [E, from REG-44, REG-45]; donor-funded buyers pay 0% VAT [V REG-44]; room nights add 3% [V REG-47].
4. **A retreat is sold as a programme,** with an agenda, outcomes and a named trainer: an employer deducts training planned in its activity plan, not entertainment [V REG-48]. A public employer plans its training by 28 February of the year before [V REG-38].
5. **Public tenders wait six months:** service tenders up to RWF 100 million are reserved for domestic companies registered for at least six months [V REG-52]; a single-source deal up to RWF 3 million needs no Umucyo account, once per supplier [V REG-52].
6. **A guest trainer who flies in to teach** needs the R special pass (RWF 50,000, up to 90 days), not the conference visa, on which work is not permitted [V REG-59, REG-60].

## Risks

1. **Heat:** no air-conditioning in a steel box; a full room in the afternoon is the first thing the pilot measures (the room's plan, open question 1).
2. **The drive:** 50 to 69 minutes each way [E LOC-19]; a group needs transport, a minibus whose hire is not priced [U].
3. **Size:** eight seats, while most tenders ask for 10 to 25 people and three or four stars [V MICE-32, MICE-34].
4. **Free and cheap courses** in Kigali: kLab's free coworking and the GIZ centre's free event space [V EDU-01, EDU-04], sponsored and free bootcamps [U EDU-13, EDU-24, EDU-28].
5. **The founder's time:** each course day is a day not spent on the products.
6. **The internet:** eight laptops, the screen and a trainer on a video call share the office's 4G router and its failover ([the office](../office/electric.md)).
7. **The rules:**
   - no paying guest before the RDB tourism licence;
   - no advertised course before RTB's written answer;
   - no paid course before the company is registered and the trainer holds a permit that allows paid work (see "Before the room opens").
8. **Later competition:** from about 2028, the airport city's conference hotels and convention centre, and the economic zone's own training institute [V LOC-57, LOC-70]; and today the free Imbuto Hub for young people's courses [V LOC-85].

## Before the room opens

Proposed on 2026-11-03, for the founder to settle [A].

The room is not on site yet [U]. A group that stays the night also needs the guest rooms, the septic's inspection and the RDB licence. The course does not have to wait for any of them:

- **The same course, at the client's office in Kigali.**
  - databayt sells the tuition alone: RWF 300,000 a course day for up to eight [A AS-33], or RWF 75,000 a person for two days.
  - The client provides the room, the screen and the Wi-Fi.
  - The farm earns nothing from these courses yet.
- **It tests the riskiest assumption first:** that a Kigali team pays for the course, before the fit-out is bought.
- **Each course finds the room's first groups.** A team that took the course in its own office is the first to be offered the two-day course with a night at the farm, once the room opens.

It waits on three things, none of them the room:

1. **The company's registration,** so that invoices carry a tax number.
2. **A permit that lets the trainer do paid work.** A visitor visa is for purposes other than work [V REG-59], and the business-entrepreneur permit needs the company's registration certificate [V REG-60].
3. **RTB's written answer** before any course is advertised (rule 1 above). Until then, courses are closed and sold to one organisation at a time.

## The first 90 days

1. **Ask RTB and RRA in writing:** does a company's short professional course need RTB's authorisation, and how are tuition, room hire, meals and room nights taxed in one package (the research's open questions)? *Both letters were drafted on 2026-11-03. RRA's waits for the company's tax number.*
2. **Price the fit-out:** quotes for the room's lines ([materials](../study-room/materials.md)), so the payback can be worked. *Shop prices were collected on 2026-11-03 (FIT-P01 to FIT-P37), so the payback above is a first range. Written quotes come next.*
3. **Write one course:** "AI at work", two days, its one-page offer in English with the three day prices. *Written on 2026-11-03, together with a one-day version, a three-hour briefing for managers and a two-day course for developers. databayt keeps them.*
4. **Collect 10 comparable venues** within two hours of Kigali: the day rate, what it includes, the date. *Nine with a published price were found on 2026-11-03 (MICE-43 to MICE-52).*
5. **Draft, do not send,** an offer to 10 prospects from the [buyers register](../../study/registers/buyers.csv): small teams in Kigali, NGOs that tendered training, the economic zone's developer and factories, the schools near the farm. The founder approves each message. *Drafted on 2026-11-03 for 20 organisations in Kigali, 8 of them from the register. None has been sent.*
6. **Run a pilot** for one friendly team at half the tuition, and measure: the room's afternoon temperature, the Wi-Fi with every laptop and the screen, the travel time, the meals, what they would pay.
7. **Settle the split** between databayt and the farm, and who holds each licence and tax registration.
8. **After 3 months,** replace each [A] above with what was measured, and record the change in the decision log.

## What to measure

- Room days sold a month, by line, and the share of weekdays filled.
- Revenue and what is left per room day.
- Beds and meals sold with each group.
- Offers sent, replies, bookings: what each booking cost to win.
- The room's temperature at 15:00 with a full table.
