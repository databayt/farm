# The farm: gas

Status: advice of 2026-10-31. The galley's fuel is not chosen yet; the gas it would burn is an estimate [E] until the menu and the range are fixed, and the prices are as published on the dates given.

Whether the galley should cook on bottled gas (LPG), how much it would burn and what it costs, and how the cylinders are stored and connected safely, for the owner and the installer.

Recommendation, in one line: cook in the galley on LPG from two large cylinders kept outside in a ventilated cage, installed by a RURA-licensed installer; keep electricity for the fridges, the extraction and the lights.

When it happens: [the farm's execution plan](plan.md) · What it changes in the power: [the farm's electric](electric.md) · The kitchen it serves: [the galley](../galley/plan.md)

## At a glance

### What the galley would burn

The method: the galley's cooking on electricity is about 17 kWh a day (25 kWh a day all electric, about 8 on gas, [the power guide](electric.md)). The heat that reaches the pots is the same on either fuel, so the gas is that heat divided by a gas burner's efficiency and by the energy in a kilogram of LPG [E, inputs in AS-30].

| What | Value | Label |
|---|---|---|
| Electric cooking it replaces | about 17 kWh a day | [E] from the power guide's loads |
| Heat that reaches the pots | about 12.8 kWh a day (17 × 75% for an electric range) | [A, AS-30] |
| LPG burned | about 2.2 kg a day (12.8 ÷ 45% burner efficiency ÷ 13.1 kWh/kg), about 65 kg a month | [E, AS-30] |
| Cylinders | about one 38 kg cylinder every 17 days, or five to six 12 kg cylinders a month | [E] |

Gas costs less than grid power at the business price and about the same at the hotel price. It also takes the galley's three-phase cooking circuits off the board and about 17 kWh a day off the farm's use, so the solar system can shrink from about 10 to about 8 kWp ([the power guide](electric.md)), and the galley keeps cooking when the grid fails.

## Hold points

1. **Cylinders outside.** No cylinder, full or empty, stands inside the galley; they stand upright in a ventilated cage outside its wall, and the gas comes in through a fixed pipe [V ENG-30].
2. **A licensed installer.** The pipe, the regulator and the change-over valve are put in by an installer holding RURA's reticulation licence, and pressure-tested before the first meal [V ENG-30].
3. **Shut-off and detection.** A shut-off valve where the pipe enters, one on each appliance, a gas detector at low level (LPG is heavier than air) and a fire blanket and extinguisher by the door.
4. **Cooking under extraction.** Every burner works under the hood the galley's electric guide already powers.

## Steps

1. Decide the galley's fuel (gas or electric) with the chef and the menu, and record it in the decision log: it changes the galley's circuits ([the galley's electric](../galley/electric.md)) and the solar size.
2. Ask two licensed LPG dealers that deliver to Bugesera for their price per kg on 38 or 50 kg cylinders, delivered, and the deposit per cylinder.
3. Ask a RURA-licensed reticulation installer to quote the cage, the pipe, the automatic change-over regulator for two cylinders, the valves and the detector.
4. Build the cage on the galley's outside wall: a concrete base at ground level, open mesh sides, a roof against sun and rain, a lock, and at least 3 m from drains and openings below ground, as RURA asks of shops [V ENG-30].
5. Choose gas-ready appliances (range, griddle, fryer) when the galley's line is bought.
6. Train the staff in RURA's leak steps: close the cylinder valve, open doors and windows, touch no switch and no phone, and call the fire brigade if it burns [V ENG-30].
7. Log every cylinder that comes in: date, size and price. After the first month, check the 2.2 kg a day against the log (AS-30).
